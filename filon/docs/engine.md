# The simulation engine (`src/sim`)

The engine is a pure, integer-only, deterministic flow simulation of one colony: entities on a 1024² tile grid are compiled into a graph of flow nodes (ribbon chains, machines, stores…) that is stepped at 10 ticks per second. The same code runs online (tick by tick) and inside the offline catch-up ([offline.md](offline.md)); a future server must be able to replay it bit for bit. No DOM, no clock, no floats in state (with the exceptions listed under *Known gaps*).

Neighbouring docs: [../ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md) (the whole app), [content.md](content.md) (the tables the engine reads), [testing.md](testing.md) (how to run and write tests), [glossary.md](glossary.md) (FR/EN game terms), [render.md](render.md) and [ui.md](ui.md) (who reads the engine), [app.md](app.md) (the shell that drives it), [extending.md](extending.md), [land.md](land.md) (the claim's land in parcels).

## Units and fixed point

Everything that enters the state is an integer (stored in `Float64Array`s or numbers, exact below 2^53).

| Quantity | Unit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Items | milli-items, `MU = 1000` per item | Stocks, buffers, parcels. Content quantities go through `toMu` ([registry.ts](../src/content/registry.ts)) |
| Flow rates | mu per tick | `mpt(perMin) = round(perMin·1000/600)`: 60/min → 100 mu/tick |
| Slow rates | mu per epoch | Drills (`rateEp`), generator burn (`burnEp`), offline throttles; consumed with a carry |
| Deposit caps | `rich` = 10 mu per epoch per tile | `rich 500` = 5 items/min/tile (`Grid.capMuPerEpoch`) |
| Power | kW | Energy in kW·tick (an accumulator of 1 200 kW·min holds 720 000 kW·tick) |
| Satisfaction, multipliers | ‰ | Power satisfaction is floored to `SAT_STEP = 25` ‰ |
| Progress | milli-ticks | Machine `p` += sat ‰ × speed ‰ / 1000 per tick; research and job `el` += sat ‰ per tick |
| Money | centi-ocelles | `Sim.credits`; archive notes are centi-◉ (they once pre-paid data; since D122 they pay tokens at CrierBelt's list price); AI tokens are whole tokens (never held: billed at each minute boundary, D68) |

Helpers in [fixed.ts](../src/core/fixed.ts): `fdiv`, `ceilDiv`, `mulDiv` (⌊a·b/c⌋, BigInt past 2^53), `isqrt` (integer Newton), `floorTo`, `clampInt`, `permille`, `dist2`, and `waterfill` (proportional water-filling + rotating leftovers; tested but **not used** by the engine, see *Known gaps*). The recurring idiom is the **carry**: `w = carry + rate·s; out = ⌊w / D⌋; carry = w − out·D` (drills and generators with `D = 600 000` for mu/epoch × ‰, machines with `D = 1000`, virtual pools with `D = 600`). Nothing is lost to rounding; the carry is part of the hashed state.

## Time

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `TPS` / `TICK_MS` | 10 / 100 | Ticks per real second |
| `EPOCH` | 600 ticks | One minute: weather steps, job re-assignment, the future market settlement |
| `CYCLE_LCM` / `PERIOD_MAX` | 1 200 / 2 400 | Recipe cycles must divide 1 200 ticks (checked by `content/validate.ts`) so steady groups have short exact periods |
| `DAY_EPOCHS` | 36 | A day: `SOLAR[day epoch]` ‰ = 12 dark, 3 dawn (150/500/850), 18 full, 3 dusk |
| `GUST_EPOCHS` / `GUST_LEVELS` | 10 / 700…1 300 ‰ | One of five gust levels per 10-epoch window, drawn by `hash32(worldSeed, window, salt)`; `gustVariance` scales the swing; part of the wind's time (D95, [Wind](#wind)) |
| `WORLD_EPOCH0` | 29 453 760 | 2026-01-01T00:00Z in minutes since the Unix epoch (≡ 0 mod 36: the world day starts at night) |

The sim only counts **ticks since founding** (`sim.tick`). The shell supplies wall-clock time: it founds a colony with `foundingEpoch = max(WORLD_EPOCH0, ⌊Date.now()/60000⌋)` ([main.ts](../src/main.ts)) and must keep ticking (or catching up) in step with real time. `sim.absEpoch(tick) = foundingEpoch + ⌊tick / EPOCH⌋` is the **absolute world epoch**: everything shared between colonies (sun via `sunPermille`, the wind via `windAt` and its gusts via `gustPermille`, later market events) is a pure function of it and of `WORLD_SEED`, never of `tick`. Everything colony-local (transit, crafting, research durations) is in ticks.

## The world

**Grid** ([grid.ts](../src/sim/world/grid.ts)). 1024×1024 tiles in 32×32 chunks, generated lazily by a pure `TerrainSource.fill(cx, cy, …)` (`MapGen` in the game, `FlatSource` in tests). A chunk holds `terrain` (a `T` class), `ore` (item + 1), `rich`, `level` (D45: rock above 0, and the Crown's tarn), plus renderer-only `height` and `biome`. Only overrides are state: `terrainDiff` (dug/filled tiles) and `oreDiff` (unused so far); both are saved and re-applied when a chunk is generated. `occ` (entity id per tile) is derived from entities. `dirtyChunks` tells the renderer what to repaint. `made0(x, y)` reads a tile as its source made it, whatever play changed since (the tiles changed keep their first value, `made`, also when a chunk is made under saved diffs): the neighbouring bases are laid out on it (D110). A source may also answer `floorAt` (open floor at level 0, cheaply, no chunk made), `keepsClear` (a rect holding what the land guarantees the claim) and `holdings` (map 4's grouped neighbouring bases).

**Terrain** ([types.ts](../src/sim/types.ts)): `Sand, Hardpan, Rock1–Rock4, Shallow, Deep, Salt, Vent, DuneSea, Dug, Filled`, and map 4's gaps `Chasm, River` (D94, appended). `isFloor` = Sand, Hardpan, Dug, Filled, Salt, Vent. `isWater` = Shallow, Deep, River (what a pump draws); `isStillWater` = Shallow, Deep; `isGap` = Chasm, River. Rock is dug to `Dug`, still water and salt flats are filled to `Filled`; a chasm and a river are neither dug nor filled; outside the world reads as `DuneSea`. **Usable floor** ([world/floor.ts](../src/sim/world/floor.ts), D94): a floor tile that some 2 × 2 square of floor at one level holds (`usableFloor`); `parcelFloor(grid, p)` counts a parcel's (‰ of its tiles), cached until the land's saved diffs grow.

**Map generation** ([mapgen.ts](../src/sim/world/mapgen.ts), data in [content/world.ts](../src/content/world.ts)). Two seeds: `WORLD_SEED` (shared) and the player's seed, and a **map version** (D63): `new MapGen(worldSeed, playerSeed, mapV)` reads that version's parameters (`MAPS[mapV]`: the cirque template, the archetypes' Crown foot, the mountains' hardness). A new base takes `MAP_VERSION` (6 since D147; the constructor's default, so the debug bases and the tests get it too), a saved one the version in its save (`SaveData.mapV`, absent: 1; [persistence.md](persistence.md)), so a base never changes land under it. `TerrainSource.mapV`, `crownOuter` (the template's own Crown foot) and `tarn` say which. An unknown version throws (a newer build's land). The chunks a `MapGen` makes are kept for the next `MapGen` of the same land and version (`MADE`, 4 096 chunks, the oldest dropped; copies go out, a grid digs its own): a base reopened, the forecast's copy, the debug bases built one after another (D132). A new version is a new `MAPS` entry, never an edit of an old one: `mapgen.test.ts` pins map 1 tile for tile against the generator of before D63, and maps 2 and 3 against the generator of before D94.

- **Map 4's obstacles** (D94; content `OBSTACLES`, `MapParams.obstacles`; map 5 keeps them, D136; maps 1–3 have none and read none of their noises). Past the Crown, the vents and the sites, `classify` gives **the sea** (`seaAt`: the shared world's, past a coast `sea.distance` tiles out along the world seed's bearing, moved by the world's noise along it; a cliff tile with fewer than two massif tiles beside it settles like the rest; nothing raised comes below level 1. `levelAt` and `terrainAt` run the same over a window of `2·raw − 2`. **The lines** ([world/obstacles.ts](../src/sim/world/obstacles.ts)) are traced once in the constructor, after the sites, in this order: the corridors (from the Crown's foot along each guaranteed site's bearing to its middle, bent every 12 tiles by up to 3, 3 tiles each side), the rivers (`drainage`), the chasms; once per land, kept for the next MapGen of the same seeds (`NETS`). A line is a polyline in quarter tiles with a half width per point (a river's segment carries its water's level a tile along, `lv`, up on a massif); `SegIndex` buckets its segments by chunk and `within` tests a tile's centre exactly in integers. The tracers walk in 1/1024 tiles on `COS64`/`SIN64`; `blocked` keeps them off the Crown (its reach + 4), the dune sea, the clearings (`keep`: the copper field's 26 tiles, `Obstacles.copper`), the corridors and the vein's ground, with room to spare. **The rivers** are a drainage network (`drainage`, content `OBSTACLES.drainage`): a lattice a node every `grid` tiles out to `radius`, each node's elevation the land's height, its massif's and mountains' raw levels weighted and a tilt to the sea's coast; nodes on the Crown, by a clearing or the vein are out, nodes in the sea its outlets. A flood from the sea in (an integer binary heap on (spill, order)) gives each land node the neighbour it drains to (`recv`), through the hollows; rain (`rain`: plain, raised, massif) gathers downstream in the reverse of the flood's order, and a stream runs from a node where `channel` has gathered, its half width by its flow (`widths`; ×`sea.estuary` into the sea), meandering on the floor (a bent midpoint). Its water's level: from a node on a massif (not a ramp) the massif's level, profiled a tile along the stretch and never rising (`profile`), the node it reaches no higher than the water arriving — a step down is a fall; on the floor 0. A hollow's bottom node `lake.depth` below its spill, carrying a stream, holds a lake. `seaPoint` is a point on the coast (the debug handle's `FL.sea`). A **chasm** — a crevasse — walks the open floor of broken ground (no raised land, no water, roughness ≥ `rugged.chasmMin`, off the rivers and the other chasms, never back over itself), its heading wandering by up to 3 64ths a step, its width pinching and widening along it (`pinch`: the shared noise read along the walk), tapered at both ends, side cracks running off it (`branch`: the same walk, narrowing to the taper; `headingOf` turns a direction into a heading); one shorter than 5 steps is dropped. **Space or ore**: the roughness (`rough`, `OBSTACLES.rugged`: a slow noise, the world's near and the player's far) lifts the massifs' and the mountains' elevation where it is high (`mountainLift`) and sinks it where it is low — open desert, broken ground —, never past middling within `Obstacles.copper` of the copper field. **Open desert** (`OBSTACLES.desert`, 2026-10-17; owner: « un petit peu plus de grands déserts ouverts, plats, assez vides de ressources, sans eau ni crevasse ni montagne »; « le cirque notamment devrait être entouré de pas mal de sable »): round the Crown `rough` holds the roughness at `low` out to `sand` (84 tiles: the two rings of parcels round the starting square) and lets it rise to the land's own by `rough` (116); wherever it is under `below` (112) the massifs' elevation and the mountains' lift (`liftOf`) drop `desertDrop` (`edge` + `drop` a unit under it: none stand there), the plains' ponds are sand, no chasm walks (`chasmMin`), the drainage lattice's nodes rise `riseEdge` + `rise` a unit (the streams go round, crossing where they must) and none lies within `rough` of the centre, and a deposit candidate is taken at `ore` ‰ of its chance (within `sand`: `sandOre` ‰, thin). The common deposits are `oreCell`'s, kept by chunk: `cells`² candidates a chunk, each taken at a chance that grows with how broken the ground round it is (16 points on two circles, each counting two: raised, a gap, water), thin on open ground, full and richer in broken ground, a vein hidden in rock at the same chance, none by the quartz vein; maps 1–3 keep their one blob a chunk. `fill` skips chasm and river tiles for the deposits. **Whole blocks** (D104, `MapParams.oreBlock`, `TerrainSource.oreBlock`): on map 4 every deposit but the quartz face — the cirque's, the copper field, the sites, the common blobs, the hidden veins — is laid in whole `oreBlock` × `oreBlock` squares on the world's even grid (`blocksOf`): as many blocks as its disc held tiles, grown as one patch from the block nearest its centre (taken at its tile's corner), each next block the one beside the patch whose distance plus a hashed wobble (`WOBBLE`, seeded by the deposit: the cirque's from the template's `shape`) is least — a natural patch, not a square — among those whose tiles all take its ore (`takesOre` on `groundAt`: rock or not without settling); the disc's richness spread over them to the unit (heart blocks full, the rest 70 %); a block lies in one chunk, and `fill` lays it whole or not at all. A thin open-ground deposit is one block or two. Tests ([mapgen.test.ts](../src/sim/world/mapgen.test.ts) « map 4 »): map 3's cirque and Crown kept, cliffs and ramps, chasms at level 0 off the Crown, a corridor to each site (a ford where a river crosses), the open sand round the cirque and the land cut up past it, the open desert, space or ore, and the rivers' drainage — streams up on the massifs, falls, never a climb along a stream's way, narrow streams and broad rivers, river tiles flowing into the sea, one sea for every claim past r 300.
- **Quartz faces farther out** (map 5, D136; content `QUARTZ_FACES`, `MapParams.faces`; owner, 2026-10-18: « il faudra mettre du quartz avec parcimonie à d'autres endroits »): map 4's land, the vein a parcel away kept, and a few short faces in the walls of the rough ground. **Land** ([world/land.ts](../src/sim/world/land.ts); the rules in [systems/land.ts](../src/sim/systems/land.ts); the design in [land.md](land.md), D60). The world is cut in square **parcels** of `PARCEL` = `BAL.land.parcel` (16) tiles, centred on the Heart: its tile is the middle of its parcel (`parcelAt(x, y)`, `parcelRect`, `parcelCentre`; `ringOf` counts the rings of parcels out from the Heart's; `PARCEL_COLS` parcels a side, the edge ones cut short). `sim.land` (`Land`) says which the claim holds:

- **The starting square** (`inStart`): every parcel within `reach` rings of the Heart's, `reach` = `BAL.land.startRings` (1: the 3 × 3 parcels of 23 tiles, ±34 tiles, about exactly the cirque and its Crown, D74). Never bought, rented nor lost; never saved.

- `holds(p)` (any of these), `covers(x, y)`, `coversFootprint(def, x, y, rot)`; `heldBeyond()` lists the parcels held past the square.
- **Neighbouring bases' parcels** (`neighbour(p)`, a colony index or −1; D110, [world/neighbours.ts](../src/sim/world/neighbours.ts) through `Land.owner`, which the Sim sets to `Sim.neighbours`): map 4's holdings of 2–4 parcels side by side, maps 1–3's scattered parcels (the sister claims' hash), past the ring round the starting square, mostly open floor (`TerrainSource.floorAt`) and off what the land guarantees (`keepsClear`); never saved; −1 on a parcel the claim holds. Each has a working base laid out lazily on the land as made (`Grid.made0`): `Neighbours.layout` (buildings, ribbons, flag), `at(x, y)` for its tiles; its chain planned from its trade and the recipes by [world/neighbour-chain.ts](../src/sim/world/neighbour-chain.ts) (`planChain`, pure) and laid until `neighbourProblems` finds nothing. Not for sale nor lease; `neighboursGiveWay` at load grants an older save the parcels where it leased or built on a base.
- **The price** (`landPrice`, centi-ocelles, from the parcel as it is now: dug rock and filled water change it): `landContent` (`BAL.land.base`, plus per tile its ground from `BAL.land.tile` — floor at `floor` where it is usable floor, else at `sliver`; a river at `river`, a chasm at `chasm`, D94 —, its ore at `depositPerValue` (250 since D94: rich ground costs a little more than open ground, not half again), plus per deposit tile the ore's value × the tile's richness × `depositPerValue`; a face ore, D87, priced as its rock), × `mods.landGrowth` ‰ once per parcel already bought, × `mods.landPrice`, × `mods.farLand` when the parcel's centre lies past `farRadius`, × the owner's `sisterMarkup` on a sister claim's parcel. `landRent` = `rentPermille` of the price × `mods.landRent` (at least 1).
- **Commands** (logged, replayed): `buyLand(p)` (refusals `none`, `held`, `credits`; a leased parcel too, its lease then ends), `rentLand(p)` (`locked` until Land Registry, `tr4`, flag `lease`; `held`; `credits`; the first hour paid at once, `next = tick + LEASE_TICKS`), `endLease(p)` (`not_leased`). `buyLand` and `rentLand` pay from `sim.credits`; all three set `dirty` and push a `land` event (`bought`, `leased`, `ended`).
- **The hour's rent** (`landEpoch`, right after `tradeEpoch` at every minute boundary, online and in the catch-up): in parcel order, each lease whose `next` has come pays its rent (`rent` event, `next += LEASE_TICKS`) or, when the purse cannot pay, **lapses** (`lapsed` event): the parcel goes back to its owner, `dirty` is set.
- **What it gates**: a `needsZone` building stands wholly on held parcels (placement, below), the Crown's rock too. One that no longer does — a lease lapsed or given up — is **dormant** (`Sim.isDormant`): no node, no power, nothing lost; holding the parcel again wakes it. Infrastructure may cross other land at the remote cost (ribbons and conduits ×`remoteBeltCostMult × mods.remoteBeltCost`) and by kite (a construction delay), never a sister claim's parcel the claim does not hold.

**No fog of war** (D148; owner, 2026-10-19: « make it so that there's no fog of war anywhere »): the whole world is known from the founding. No rule reads what the claim has seen — placing, marking, buying and leasing, the deposit card and the land panel's counts read any tile — and nothing is saved for it. D117's fog (`Sim.explored` discs lifted round the Core, the parcels held and the buildings stood up) went with it; a save's discs are dropped on load (save v23).

## Entities

`Ent` ([types.ts](../src/sim/types.ts)) is `{ id, def, x, y, rot, cfg, portItems?, builtAt }`. Ids come from `Entities.nextId` and are never reused; `ents.sorted()` (by id) is the canonical order for compilation and hashing. For a system that runs every epoch on a big base, `ents.ofKinds(kinds)` is the same order narrowed to some building kinds and `ents.defCounts()` the count per def; all three are cached until the next add or remove (a `def` never changes in place). `x, y` is the top-left of the *rotated* footprint; `footprint`, `rotatePoint` and `worldPorts` ([entities.ts](../src/sim/entities.ts)) rotate by index swapping (no trig). Each world port knows its tile, its direction and the outside tile it faces.

- `cfg` ([cfg.ts](../src/sim/cfg.ts)): machines keep `recipe index + 1` in bits 0–15 (`recipeOf`, `withRecipe`); bits 16–19 held the speed modules, gone since D81 (`modulesOf` / `withModules` serve only the load, which hands them back as servomotors); forks keep one output filter per side, `item + 1` in byte `side` (N 0, E 1, S 2, W 3; 0 = anything). Always go through these helpers.
- `portItems`: per-port item served by an outlet, `-1` = closed. No building of today's has outlets to set: the stores that had them, the waystation and the skydocks, are retired (D60, [D108](decisions.md)); `setPortItem` and its `portItem` command stay for older logs, A trade post has none: its one exit hands out everything it bought, in turn (D62), and `setPortItem` refuses it. `setPortItem` refuses a port that is not an `out` port, so the Hearth — inputs only, it takes and never gives (D34) — has none to set. Silos have none either: a silo is single-item and every outlet serves its locked item (the unloader, which added a virtual port of its chosen item, is retired: D117).
- `builtAt`: 0 = built; otherwise the tick at which a remote construction completes (`build.completeConstructions`, first thing in `step`). Until then the entity occupies its tiles but has no node and no power; its wires are kept but carry nothing until it stands.
- Dormant entities also get no node (`isDormant`): their unlock was given up (a reconversion, from before D107), or they need land and stand off the claim's (a lease lapsed or given up, D60).

## The Sim facade, commands and events

`Sim` ([state.ts](../src/sim/state.ts)) owns the state (identity and clock, the terrain and the land held (`sim.land`), Hearth stock and `stores`, accumulator energy `accEnergy`, research, space, the compiled graph) and delegates rules to the systems. Every player action is a **command method** (`place`, `remove`, `setRecipe`, `setPortItem`, `setForkFilter`, `setModule`, `grant`, `grantCredits`, `unlockPlan`, `startResearch`, `cancelResearch`, `queueResearch`, `setTokenPlan`, `respec`, `designate`, `upgradeHearth`, `buyLand`, `rentLand`, `endLease`, `sellLand`, `setFloorPrice`, `link`, `unlink`, `nameColony`, `setEmblem` (the claim's emblem, D58: cosmetic, saved, not hashed), `completeQuest`, the blueprints' library `saveBlueprint` / `renameBlueprint` / `deleteBlueprint` (D154: saved, not hashed)): it validates, applies at once (there is no queue: a command issued between two steps takes effect at the next `step`), and appends `{ tick, cmd }` to `sim.log` only when accepted. `exec(sim, cmd)` maps a logged `Command` back onto these methods; `replay(sim, log, toTick)` re-runs a log on a fresh colony — guarded by [commands.test.ts](../src/sim/commands.test.ts) (same log ⇒ same hash). The `drifter`, `route` and `unroute` commands stay in the union so older logs still read; `exec` returns false for them, as for `respec` (the skydocks are retired, [D108](decisions.md)). Anything that mutates a colony outside commands (tests poking `sim.done` — `testSim()` learning every plan, `steadyPower`'s en5 —, `buildBench`, sandbox helpers) breaks replay unless the replaying colony starts alike.

The engine never produces text. It reports through `sim.events` (`structure`, `epoch`, `research`, `plan`, `dug`, `filled`, `land`, …), which the shell drains every frame ([app.md](app.md)). Internal flags: `dirty` (recompile before the next tick), `jobsDirty` (idle excavators/backfillers look for work), `grid.dirtyChunks`.

## Placement and replacement

[systems/build.ts](../src/sim/systems/build.ts). `canPlace(def, x, y, rot)` returns a `PlaceCheck {ok, reason, cost, delay, replaces}`. The checks run in this order: the Hearth is `not_buildable`; then `locked`; then, tile by tile, `outside_world`, occupancy (`occupied`), level ground (`uneven`: every tile at the first one's `grid.level`, D45) and `terrain` (rock passes for an `onRock` building, D44); `chasm` / `river` for a footprint tile in a gap (D94: the `terrain` refusal by name); then `needs_deposit` (a face ore is no drill's deposit, D87) / `needs_sand` / `needs_face` (a face cutter whose back meets no face ore: [face.ts](../src/sim/face.ts) `faceTiles`, D87) / `needs_water` / `needs_vent`, and `terrain` again for an `onRock` footprint only partly on rock; then the land ([land.md](land.md)): `outside_zone` when a `needsZone` building does not stand wholly on held parcels (on rock too: D44's exemption went with D60), `neighbour_land` when anything not wholly on held land touches a neighbouring base's parcel (D110), unless Neighbour Treaties are learnt (`neighbourCrossing`) or it is a camouflaged ribbon (`BuildingDef.camo`, D109); a tile under a neighbouring base's building, ribbon or flag (`Sim.neighbours.at`) is `occupied` with the occupancy, whatever the treaties; then the cost (ribbons and conduits not wholly on held land ×`remoteBeltCostMult × mods.remoteBeltCost`; a camouflaged ribbon ×`BAL.camoRemoteCostMult`, 1), the trade post `limit`, the steps (D77: a line piece facing the tile right ahead on another level — `needs_lift` for a plain ribbon of any tier, `steep` for a traction ribbon two levels or more away, `cliff` for a conduit facing a step up or a drop of two (D114: one terrace down it pours); a skybridge is not checked; `stepRefusal`), `lift_room` (a traction step right after or right before another on its line: an elevator needs a flat ribbon tile in front of it, D65), `junction_deck` (D158: a junction's deck — along its rotation's axis, `deckSides`: north–south for rot 0 and 2, east–west for 1 and 3 — comes down only on a line piece, `deckEndOk`: a ribbon of any tier, a traction or buried ribbon, a skybridge end; `deckBlocks` reads it both ways, a junction with something else at a deck end and anything of any footprint at a junction's deck end, another junction included; its tunnel's sides take anything, so junctions chain along their tunnels; what stood there before the rule stays), `wind_spacing` (D132: a wind turbine whose middle stands closer than `BAL.wind.spacing` tiles, 6, to another turbine's, read off the occupancy round it — `windNear`; one it replaces does not count, a pitch-control turbine laid over a wind turbine), `bridge_over_tall` (D124: a skybridge whose span to its partner crosses a tall building, or a tall building with a tile under a pair's span; the height class is `BUILDING_FLAT`: ribbons, routing blocks, junctions, bridges, conduits and fences are flat, all else tall; pairs laid before stay), `unaffordable`, and `reserve` (the remote surcharge, which no removal gives back, must leave the restart reserve whole). The `delay` is the remote construction time: 0 on held land, else the straight distance from the Heart's tile at `BAL.remoteBuildTilesPerSec` × `mods.remoteBuildDelay` ‰.

**Replacement** (Mindustry-style, decision D24). An occupied tile is no refusal when the entity standing there may be replaced (`replaceable`):
- it has the same non-empty `replaceGroup` as the new building (content: `transport` = every ribbon tier, the routing blocks, the junction and the bridge; `fluid` = conduits; `power` = masts; `drill` = drills). Machines, stores and the Hearth have no group, so they never replace and are never replaced;
- it lies **wholly** inside the new footprint. Partly covering a bigger entity is `occupied`;
- it is not the very same building: same def, same origin, and the same rotation where rotation matters (`turns`: ports, line pieces, directional routing blocks, non-square footprints, a junction's deck — D158: a junction turned a quarter is another junction, half round the same one). Re-placing an identical building is `occupied`. So is a distributor or mast placed again with another rotation, since that would change nothing.

Several entities can be replaced at once: a distributor over four ribbons and routers, a deep bore over a drill. `replaces` lists their ids in ascending order; it is empty on free ground, and it is also filled when only the cost refuses (`unaffordable`), so that a preview can net the refund. **Affordability counts the refund**: for each item, the stock plus the refund of every replaced entity must cover the new cost. A refund is what the building cost when it went up (`refundCost`, [refund.ts](../src/sim/refund.ts), D123): today's price (`costOf`), or, for an entity below the colony's `costFloor`, its price before D123 (content/past-costs.ts). `removeEntity` always refunds exactly that.

`place` takes the replaced entities down first (`removeEntity(id, silent)` in ascending id order, with full refunds of cost, buffers, modules and store; their ribbon parcels go back to the Hearth at the next recompile, or onto the new chain when a ribbon replaces a ribbon). It then pays, adds the entity, logs **one** `place` command and pushes **one** `structure` event. A replay re-decides the same replacement from the grid. When `cfg` is omitted and the single replaced entity has the same def (a sorter turned round), its settings are carried over through `validCfg`; otherwise a new machine starts on its **lone recipe** (`loneRecipe`, D83): the only one of its recipes unlocked at that moment (a wire mill on copper wire; a smelter, with plates and glass to choose from, waits for the player). Explicit settings win (a replay passes the logged ones). The kept `cfg` is the one that is logged. The rotation is `placementRot`'s: a one-tile directional routing block dropped onto a ribbon takes the way the items enter that tile (`entryRot`, D98: its back to the feed, so on a curve its front and sides cover the way out) unless the player turned it in hand (`turned`: R wins; the `place` command then carries `turned: true`, logged only where it changed the rotation), and a face cutter (D87) turns its back to the quartz wall it is dropped against (`faceRot`: the asked turn, else the first clockwise whose back meets a face ore), and a junction whose deck would come down on something else turns its deck to the other axis when that one fits (D158), unless turned; the turn taken is the one logged, and the ghost shows it.

**Ribbon drags** ([systems/belt-plan.ts](../src/sim/systems/belt-plan.ts), D73). `planBelts(def, path)` is a pure planner that sees the drag whole. It returns one `BeltStep` per path tile: `{x, y, rot, def, skip, leapt?, …PlaceCheck}`. The build tool's ghost shows it while the drag runs ([input.md](input.md)), and on release `placeBelts` lays exactly the steps it accepts, each as its own logged `place`, so a replay needs no planner. Tile by tile:
- this very ribbon, in the same direction: `skip`;
- a non-line block of the ribbon's group (router, sorter, gate, distributor, junction, bridge end) in a drag of two tiles or more: kept (`skip`, `def` = what is there). A one-tile drag (a click) replaces it, if it fits inside the tile;
- **auto-junction** (axis-symmetric, checked 2026-10-17 in [junction-axes.test.ts](../src/sim/junction-axes.test.ts): every drag way across every line way, three tiers, by hand and by drag): a ribbon of any tier running perpendicular to the drag, at a tile the drag runs straight through (the previous tile is behind it, in the same direction, and the next is ahead; never the first or last tile, never a bend) becomes a junction (the building of kind `crossing`, when unlocked), so both lines keep flowing. Its deck runs along the line it crosses (D158: turned as that ribbon), so the drag runs under it — a drag across lines side by side lays a row of junctions and its items pass under each in turn; along the drag when only that axis comes down on line pieces (a router at the crossed line's next tile);
- **a tee or a join** (D152, conduit drags only, two tiles or more): the first tile on a conduit the drag leaves by its side, or the last on a conduit it enters from the side, is kept as it runs (`skip`, its own `rot`, `tee` `'split'` / `'join'`) — the new line draws water off it there, or pours into it — when that conduit hands its water on (`goesOn`: the middle of its chain, or a head with an out-edge); one that ends there still turns into the drag (a bend), as a one-tile drag still turns any;
- a plain ribbon (of a ribbon drag, not a conduit's) that would face a step (`needs_lift`) becomes a **traction ribbon** once its plan is learnt, whatever the tier dragged (it takes its line's pace, D141); one already on the step, the same way, is kept (`skip`); two planned steps in a row leave the later one `lift_room`;
- otherwise the dragged ribbon, as `canPlace` decides (another tier or another direction is replaced).

Then what the grid cannot see before anything is laid: a planned junction at another planned junction's deck end is `junction_deck` (D158; along their tunnels they chain). Each run of tiles refused as `occupied`, `terrain`, `chasm`, `river` (D94), `neighbour_land` (D110) or `junction_deck` is then leapt by a **skybridge pair** standing on the plain ribbon tiles just before and after it (`leap`) — taking in the planned junctions right before or after the run, since a bridge end never stands on one (a row of junctions by a pond is leapt whole) — when the bridge is learnt, the run is straight with each tile facing on, the start is fed from straight behind (a corner would feed it from its side, D55), the ends are within `4 + mods.bridgeSpan` on one level, no other bridge of that direction stands or is planned on the line within reach (it would take the leap), and both ends pass `canPlace`. The tiles between are `skip` and `leapt` (`def`: what stands there, or `''`), left as they are; the compiler pairs the two ends as for any bridge. A pair never leaps a **tall building** (D124): the tiles holding one are refused `bridge_over_tall` and the drag is laid in part up to them (the planner does not re-route: the player drags round). A run no pair can leap stays refused, and the drag is laid in part. Ribbons only: no junction, lift or bridge carries fluids.

After the bridges, **what the land still refuses is buried** (D109): a tile of the dragged flat ribbon (not a traction, conduit or camouflaged drag) still refused as a neighbouring base's land becomes the **camouflaged ribbon** once `xp_camo` is learnt, when `canPlace` lets it there (or refuses it only `unaffordable` / `reserve`: the ghost then says what is short). After the bridges, so a run a pair can leap keeps the line's pace; a plain ribbon on free land stays plain (it costs nothing even at the remote rate). `landReason(sim, x, y)` names the land a tile is on for the build tool's tip (the neighbour's, free, or null on held land).

The steps share one budget in path order: a drag that runs short of materials is `unaffordable` from the tile where laying it would stop.

## Blueprints (D154)

[systems/blueprint.ts](../src/sim/systems/blueprint.ts). No new rule for placing: a paste is a sequence of the player's commands, each logged, so a replay needs no blueprint and every rule, cost, refund and kite applies as by hand.

- **A blueprint** (`Blueprint {id, name, icon, w, h, pieces, wires}`): pieces `{def, x, y, rot, recipe?, filters?}` by **content id** (building, recipe, a routing block's items: a router's or distributor's per world side, a sorter's or gate's one item), their place from the corner of the footprints taken, in row order; `wires` the power wires between two pieces (indices). `capture(sim, box)` takes every building touching an inclusive box but the Core (a building half in it comes whole); `icon` is `mainBuilding` (the commonest that works — not a line piece, a pole or a store —, the dearer on a tie). At most `MAX_PIECES` (2 000).
- **Turned and mirrored** (`layout`): mirrored left ⇄ right first, then turned `turn` quarter turns clockwise, the blueprint's middle on the cursor as `originFor` puts a building's. A piece's rotation follows; a router's or distributor's sides turn with it (`sideAfter`). A mirror needs each building's **twin** (`mirrorRot`: the rotation whose footprint and ports — tile, side, way in or out — are the mirror image's; line pieces, routing blocks and port-less buildings take the mirrored way); `mirrorBlocker` names the first that has none, and the paste is then not mirrored (most 2 × 2 machines: in and out at one corner).
- **Planned** (`planPaste` → `planPieces`): each piece through `placementRot` (with `turned`: a routing block keeps the blueprint's way over a ribbon) and `canPlace`. A piece where this very building already stands, turned alike (`turns`), is `kept` (free; its settings applied). `unaffordable` alone is no refusal: the pieces that fit are summed in **one budget**, net of the refund of what they replace (`refundCost`), into `cost`, and `short` is what the Core lacks of it. Pieces refused for anything else are `skipped`, the first reason kept. Unknown ids (since retired; `RENAMED` followed) are dropped and counted (`gone`).
- **Laid** (`lay`): **nothing when short** (paid whole or not at all). Else a kept piece's settings by `setRecipe` / `setForkFilter`; then the pieces that fit by `place(def, x, y, rot, cfg, turned)` — those that replace something first (their refund comes in), then the buildings, the poles, the line pieces, blueprint order within each (a piece `place` refuses then, its ground changed by an earlier one, is skipped too); then the wires by `link` (the pillar as the end it starts from). A new pole wires itself to the nearest point as by hand (D25). Off the claim's land the pieces fly out by kite (`builtAt`). `paste(sim, bp, hx, hy, turn, mirror)` is both.
- **The upgrade of an area** (`planUpgrade`, `upgradeArea`): each line piece touching the box that has a faster tier unlocked (`nextTier`; the tiers from the content: per kind, the pieces that are only faster — not a lift, not buried, not glazed — by `belt.perMin`: ribbon → swift → express; the one conduit has none) is planned as that tier over it, through the same plan and `lay` (`place` replaces it by its group and refunds it). `upgradeOpen`: is a tier above the slowest unlocked.
- **The library**: `Sim.blueprints`, saved as `blueprints` (optional; absent when empty; `FIELDS` plain; read back through `cleanBlueprint`, a malformed one dropped), not hashed. `saveBlueprint(bp, name)` (a copy with the next id; `bpSave`), `renameBlueprint` (`bpRename`), `deleteBlueprint` (`bpDelete`); a name `cleanName`d (one line, `NAME_MAX` 40). `blueprintCost` for the library's chips.
- **Cost** (the late base, 225 pieces, [bench/blueprint-perf.test.ts](../src/sim/bench/blueprint-perf.test.ts)): a plan 0.6 ms, a paste 2.0 ms, the next tick's recompile 2.5 ms (CPU).

## Graph compilation

`compileGraph` ([compile.ts](../src/sim/graph/compile.ts)) turns entities into a `Graph` (`nodes`, `chains`, edge arrays `eSrc/eDst/eSrcPort/eDstPort`, `nodeByEnt`, `chainByTile`, `nets`, `reservoirs`, `hearth`). `Sim.ensureCompiled()` runs it whenever `dirty` is set (any structural command, construction, research change, job start/end, owed data paid).

1. **Ribbon tiles** (`belt`, `conduit`, `bridge` kinds, built only) are indexed by tile, and so are the junctions (kind `crossing`). A junction passes items straight across, so whatever faces one faces the first tile past it, in line (`across`), past a whole row of them (D158: junctions chain along their tunnels; the line has a cell under each, D121). This holds for a ribbon's target, an outlet, and a routing block's side, on both axes: the engine reads no junction's rotation, which only decides which line rides over (the deck rule is placement's, D158). A junction dragged across the line next to a router or a machine never cuts it.
2. **One node per building** (by id, skipping unbuilt and dormant ones), via `reuse(e, make, same)`: the previous node of the entity is kept (with its buffers) when it is still the same kind of thing (same recipe, same ore…), otherwise its contents are refunded to the Hearth (`refundNode`) and a new node is made. A recipe change refunds its machine's buffers at once, in the command (`setRecipe`), not at the next compile: a change and its undo with no compile between them would otherwise keep the buffers where one compile between refunds them, and looking at the sim (`hash()` compiles) must never change what it does (replay; `commands.test.ts`). Ports are registered: in-ports by `tile·4 + dir`, out-ports with the tile they face (past junctions). Forks get **dynamic ports**: each side with a ribbon (past junctions) is an input if that ribbon points in, an output otherwise.
3. **Targets and feeders**: each ribbon tile targets the tile ahead — a skybridge leaps to the next skybridge ahead (on its own level) within `4 + mods.bridgeSpan`, and is entered from straight behind only (a ribbon or a routing block at its side does not feed it, [D55](decisions.md)); crossings are skipped, so both lines pass straight through. **Levels (D45)**: a ribbon's target must stand on its own level, a traction ribbon's (`lift`) one level up or down, a conduit's on its level or one below (D114: water pours down a terrace, never up); an outlet faces only its own level, a routing block meets ribbons and buildings of its own level (a traction ribbon may climb into it). A cliff ends a line (placement no longer lays a piece that faces one, D77; lines laid before stay). The target is a ribbon of the same fluid class that does not point back, or an in-port facing it. Ribbons and out-ports facing a ribbon count as its feeders.
   **Tees** (D152; owner, 2026-10-19: « we also need conduits that share the water between multiple paths »): then a conduit tile that nothing feeds — no conduit runs into it, no out-port hands into it — draws its water off a conduit beside it on its level that runs across the edge between them: the one behind it first, else on its left, else its right; never off one running the same way beside it, nor off one that draws from it; tiles in ascending order. That conduit is a tee (`Graph.tees`: tile → the sides it hands water out of; `Graph.drawsFrom`: branch → tee); the branch counts it as its feeder and is fed from that side (`feedDirs`). A drag off a pipe's side makes one (below, the drag's `tee`).
   **Pressure gauges** (D155): a gauge (content `gauge`, kind `conduit`) has no pace of its own: `paceAt` gives it its conduit's tier, so a line through it stays one chain (its state kept across the swap). Placement: onto a conduit only (`needs_conduit`), turned as it runs (`placementRot`); never dragged (`isDragDef`); a conduit drag along the line keeps it. Its reading is [readouts/pressure.ts](../src/sim/readouts/pressure.ts): a network's pumps (`rateEp` × the grid's satisfaction) against its drinkers at full work, ‰, states by `BAL.pressure`; read only, never state.
4. **Chains**: tile F continues into G when G is F's target, of the same tier (building def) and F is G's **only** feeder, and F is no tee (D152: a tee ends its chain). A skybridge pair has no pace of its own: its chain takes the tier of the line feeding it (`paceAt`, [D55](decisions.md)). A traction ribbon keeps its tier (its chain is its own, so a saved line's chains and items load as they were) but not its pace (D141, `linePace`): its chain goes at the pace of the fastest ribbon it meets on its line — the ribbons feeding it and the one it hands on to, through bridges and other traction ribbons; by pace, then building index — and never under its own `belt` (a plain ribbon's 60/min). The ribbon paces divide each other (60, 120, 240) on the world's clock, so a lift steps whenever its line does and a full line moves through it as one block. Chains are traced from tiles without a predecessor (in tile order), then pure loops (broken at their smallest tile). So a tile fed from several sides starts a new chain: merges and side-loads always happen at a chain's tail. **Cells over a leap (D121)**: a tile that leaps — a skybridge's start over its gap, a ribbon before junctions — is followed in its chain by a cell for each tile it passes over (`spanTile(tile, way)` in [chain.ts](../src/sim/flow/chain.ts): coded past the world's tiles, `SPAN_TILE × (1 + way) + tile`, so the two lines through a junction have a cell each there; `isSpanTile`, `tileUnder`, `Chain.lastTile`: its last world tile, whose target the head hands on to). An item crosses a leap a tile a step, as on the ribbons — the leap's real length in time, the line's throughput — and a line holds an item over each tile of it (owner, 2026-10-17: « la vitesse sur le bridge, la junction, est plus rapide que sur les tapis d'entrée et de sortie »).
5. **Contents survive**: a chain with the same fingerprint (`chainKey`: tier, length, first, middle and last tile) and the very same tiles copies its state; otherwise each ribbon item stays on its own tile (`collectCells`: a recut line keeps every item where it stands, its stage's part with its tail tile), and a conduit's runs are spread on their tiles item by item (`collectParcels`: each item at its own arrival in its run's window and never closer to the head than the items ahead of it; a jammed ribbon's coalesced head run holds them all), so each part of a recut ribbon keeps the items standing on it and re-pushed onto the new chains; parcels left on tiles that are no longer ribbons are refunded to the Hearth. Nothing vanishes.
6. **Edges**: a chain head feeds the next chain (priority 0 when straight, 1 when side-loading) or the in-port it faces, and a tee's head then each of its branches, N E S W (D152); a closed loop feeds itself; an out-port facing a ribbon feeds that chain; an out-port set against an in-port feeds it directly, no ribbon needed (D39: machines, drills, stores, the Heart; only for nodes with no other output). **At most one edge per port**; only a chain's tail takes several inputs, sorted by priority then creation order.
7. **Power grids**: see *Power*.

Compilation is a full rebuild with state carried over (the 25 k-entity bench compiles in about 60–90 ms), not an incremental recompile around changed tiles. Determinism comes from sorted entities, sorted tiles and head-first chain order.

### Routing blocks (kind `fork`)

Mindustry-style distribution blocks, all built from the start (the distributor with dn5). They have no fixed ports: the compiler links each to what surrounds it, within the sides its logic allows (`canIn` / `canOut` in compile.ts) — the distributor takes from and gives to every side; directional blocks (the router, sorter, inverted sorter, overflow and underflow gates, placed with a rotation) take from their back only and give to their front and both sides (the router too since D35). A one-tile directional block placed onto a ribbon takes the way the items enter that tile (`placementRot` → `entryRot` in systems/build.ts, also used by the ghost; D98): its back faces the feed the compiler links — a ribbon pointing in (past junctions, a traction ribbon from one level off, never a bridge leaping over), an outlet, a routing block — straight behind first, then a side; so on a curve the block's side takes the way out. A rotation the player chose with R wins (`turned`).

- **Ribbons**: a ribbon pointing in is an input, any other adjacent ribbon an output (the routing block side-loads onto it); conduits never connect.
- **Buildings**: a machine's or store's input port facing the block → an output edge; an output port facing it → an input edge (pass 2b, `links`).
- **Other routing blocks**: block A gives to block B where A may give on that side and B may take on the facing one (two distributors link both ways, as in Mindustry; two routers only front to back).
- **Stores**: a routing block takes from a store's outlet facing it like any other port; the unloader, which pulled a chosen item out of the store it touched through a virtual port, is retired (D117): a silo holds one kind and its outlets empty it by themselves, and the Hearth never gives (D34).
- After all edges exist, each `ForkNode` gets `fronts` / `sides` (its out-edges by side, from the port index `% 4` vs the entity's `rot`) and its per-edge `filters`. Port indices on a routing block are `(tile index in the footprint) × 4 + side`, so the distributor (2×2) has eight sides.
- The graph also exports `feedDirs` (per ribbon tile, a mask of the sides it is fed from: ribbons, ports, routing blocks, bridges) — the views draw ribbon bends from it, so what is drawn is what is connected.

## The flow step

**Nodes** ([node.ts](../src/sim/flow/node.ts), [nodes.ts](../src/sim/flow/nodes.ts), [chain.ts](../src/sim/flow/chain.ts)). Every node extends `FNode` (`inE`/`outE`, `net`, `group`, `status`, `kw`/`idleKw`) and implements `offer/accept/split/take/give/update/demand/hash/snapshot/restore`.

| Node | Used for (building kind) | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `Chain` | ribbons, swift/express/traction ribbons, skybridges, conduits | **Ribbons go tile by tile** (D57, owner's design, 2026-10-13): one cell per tile (`cells`: item + 1, 0 empty, tail to head). On its tier's **step** (`stepAt(perMin, now)`: perMin steps a minute on the world's clock, the same beat for every ribbon of that pace — 10 ticks for a ribbon, 5 swift, 2 or 3 express; a traction ribbon at its line's, 10 at least (D141); `stepPeriod`) every item moves one cell on if the cell ahead is free (head first, so a packed block moves as one); the head offers its item only on a step, whole. A chain whose first cell is taken still accepts an item on its step when the block from its tail will move up (`willShift`: a free cell ahead, or its head taken this step), which needs the chain ahead to have decided first: the tick accepts the ribbons downstream first (`Graph.accOrder`, step.ts), so a line moves as one block across its joins. Where lines meet (a chain's tail) the straight feeder has the cell first, then the side-loads. Items enter whole (onto the first cell, placed in phase C after the step) — a feeder's part of an item (a stray part, an old save) is loaded in a **stage** at the tail until whole. Throughput = the beat (one item a step); capacity one item per tile. Render-only bookkeeping, not hashed: `moved` (tick an item reached its cell), `via` (the in-edge it came by, `VIA_BEHIND`), `lastOut`. At night (`ctx.glean`) a raid of the Gleaners takes an item a post off its exposed cells (`raidStep`, [below](#the-gleaners-d103)). **Conduits** keep a FIFO of runs `{item, mu, from, to}` (the window in which a run reaches the head), throughput `thrMu`, transit `tiles × ticksPerTile`, `conduitDensity` 4 a tile, runs of one item merging within 2 s (`runSpanTicks`). Fluids only on conduits. A conduit whose head is a **tee** (D152) has several out-edges and shares what its head offers among them (`shareFluid`, below), counting each way's take in `shared` (the inspector's bars; never hashed or saved) |
| `MachineNode` | `machine` (kiln, workshops, mills, ovens, charger…) | Inputs consumed at cycle start, integer progress `p += ⌊(carry + s·speed)/1000⌋` towards `ticks·1000`, outputs emitted only if they fit (`Blocked` holds `p` at the end). Buffers: 2 crafts in, 3 out. `speed` from `craftSpeed`, speed modules, tower, dug floor. Machines without `kw` (the kiln burns coal as an input) run unpowered. **Waste (D125)**: a recipe with `waste` counts its crafts (`wc`, 0 … every − 1); the craft that completes the count also drops one waste item into the **waste bin** (`wBuf`, `wItem`, room `BAL.wasteBufItems` = 2), and a craft that would not fit it waits (`Blocked`, `wasteFull()`). The bin goes out **first, through the product's own output**: `offer` hands a whole waste item while the bin holds one, the product otherwise, so the line carries both in turn and a waste at the head of a line into a machine that does not take it, or into the Core, which takes none (D139), jams the line like any wrong item (a sorter and an incinerator, or a filtered router side, free it). An empty bin holds no item (its state is its contents: the catch-up compares states). The count and the bin are hashed only where there is any, saved in the node's snapshot (`MachineSnap.wc`, `wBuf`, `wItem`; a save from before D125 restores the buffers with an empty bin and a fresh count), refunded with the rest (`refundNode`, `setRecipe`), counted in `totalHeld`; the waste is counted in `produced` when it drops into the bin |
| `DrillNode` | `drill`, deep bore, `pump` (water, 60/min by water) | Continuous rate `rateEp × sat` with a carry, capped by the deposit tiles beneath (majority ore, Σ tile caps × `capMult`, ring-3 bonus), floored to a quarter item a minute (`DRILL_STEP_MU`, D138: whole items in a short cycle), 5-item buffer |
| `ReservoirNode` | Hearth, silo (`storage`, single item), research lab (`lab`), server (`server`: power only, no stock), excavator, backfiller, trade post | A stock with a per-item capacity, per-port outlets handing out one whole item on each of their beats (`portPerMin`, from `portRate` mu/tick: the ribbons' `stepAt`, D57; a part only when that is all the store holds), optional `accepts` filter (the Core: every item but kind `waste`, `CORE_TAKES` — D139: slag and scrap stop at its intake from a ribbon, a routing block or a machine set against it, and the line backs up; labs and servers: nothing, no stock — power-only, D122; excavators: nothing; backfillers: stone), optional `cycle` (a trade post's exit, D62: the items it bought, one each beat in turn — the beat's index on the world clock picks it, a beat whose item is out passes — so each item flows as a one-item outlet would; `portPulls` names them for the catch-up). The Hearth's stock array **is** `sim.stock`; other stocks are `sim.stores` entries. Never accepts fluids. Has a virtual mode for the catch-up |
| `ForkNode` | `fork` | The routing blocks (router, sorter, inverted sorter, overflow / underflow gate, distributor): a 2-item buffer (×w·h) holding one item kind, handed on by `mode` (`BuildingDef.logic`): routers and distributors round-robin to every output, with a filter per side from the start (`filters`: an output with a filter takes only its item, and that item goes there first); sorters send their item to the `fronts` and others to the `sides` (inverted: the opposite); gates fill `fronts` then `sides` (underflow: the opposite). An item no output takes blocks the inputs. A routing block laid over a ribbon tile keeps the item that stood there (D139, compile.ts `intoFork`: a sorter set down where a slag waits at a door takes it in) |
| `GeneratorNode` | steam boiler, battery generator, sunheart (`generator`; the coal generator until D143) | Fuel arrives as an ordinary item flow (½ min buffer); burns `burnEp × dispatch` per epoch with a carry. A steam boiler (D114, content `gen.water`) has a second box, its water (`waterItem`, `waterEp`), fed by conduit and drunk the same way with its own carry; it gives power only with both in hand (`fueled`), and lacking either it waits (`NoFuel`), so the solve, the night's cover, the Électricité view and the catch-up treat it as any fuel generator. A generator without water hashes and saves as before (`water`/`wCarry` only when it drinks) |
| `ConsumerNode` | none today (the trade post's until M6) | Draws `idleKw`, no flows |
| `SinkNode` | incinerator (D51) | Takes whatever its feeds offer, whole, and destroys it (counted in `consumed`); stateless, never backs a line up. Render-only, neither hashed nor saved (D125, like a ribbon's `lastOut`): `burnTick`, `burnItem`, `burnPort`, `burns` — the last item it destroyed and how many since the compile, which the pit's burst ([render.md](render.md)) and its sound read; `restore` (a catch-up's warm-up rolled back) clears `burnTick` |

Wind flowers, solar panels, geothermal plants and accumulators are not nodes: they are producers/accumulators of their grid. Masts and crossings have no node.

**Phases** ([step.ts](../src/sim/flow/step.ts) `runPhases`). Each tick, over a node list:

- **A1 offers**: every source node states, per out-edge, the item and amount it could send (start-of-tick state only).
- **A2 accepts**: every destination splits its free room among its incoming offers.
- **A3 splits**: every source splits what it has among its out-edges, knowing what they accept → `q[e]`.
- **B transfers**: destination by destination, in-edges in priority order: `src.take`, `dst.give`.
- **C updates**: internal progress (crafting, drilling, burning, a ribbon's step and the item it took in, a conduit's head coalescing, silo unlock).

Every allocation reads start-of-tick state, so the result does not depend on node order — guarded by "runs the same whatever order the nodes are processed in" ([flow.test.ts](../src/sim/flow/flow.test.ts)) — with one designed exception: a full ribbon's accept reads whether its own head was taken this step, so the accepts run in `Graph.accOrder` (every other node first, then the ribbons downstream first: `depth`, a loop cut where the walk meets itself; offline, each group's `accOrder`).

**Sharing** is greedy from a **rotating start** (`(k + now % n) % n`, `allocRotating`): round-robin over ticks for machine inputs, reservoir in/outlets, multi-output drills and machines. Routing blocks deal out **per item** instead: `ForkNode.turn` moves on each tick something left (kept modulo `turnMod`, the lcm of its output sets), since whole items arrive at a steady pace and a tick-based turn fell on the same output every time (D37). Chain tails are **priority** merges: the straight feeder has the first cell first, side-loads only when it does not take it (owner, 2026-10-13: "the ribbon that doesn't turn would have the priority"). **A tee's water** (D152) is shared evenly instead (`shareFluid` in [flow/node.ts](../src/sim/flow/node.ts)): the head's offer split among the tee's ways, each capped by what it accepts, a way that takes less handing the rest to the others round after round (max-min fair), the remainder a milli-item a way in turn from `now % n` — every way some each tick, so each branch stays one stream (greedy turns would cut a conduit's runs every other tick). The water-filling helper of `core/fixed.ts` is not used. Rotation depends on `now % n`, and the ribbons' and outlets' beats on `now % stepPeriod`: the catch-up folds both into a group's period (`Group.rotL`).

**Back-pressure**: a full chain accepts nothing, a machine whose output does not fit stops (`Blocked`), a drill with a full buffer stops, a full Hearth refuses (its cap is per item, ×2 per level). Nothing is destroyed: refunds may push the Hearth above its cap. An item the Core **auto-sells** (D127, [below](#auto-sell-d127)) keeps the cap since D163: its threshold stays under it, and the surplus is sold at the minute's end. `Building` and `Dormant` are derived by the UI from `builtAt` and `isDormant`; reservoirs never set a status. Statuses drive `demand()` (working `kw` vs `idleKw`), so they are dynamic state.

## Power

Power goes through explicit **wires** (decision D25, Mindustry's power nodes): nothing is powered by proximity.

- **Wires** ([links.ts](../src/sim/power/links.ts)): `sim.links` (`PowerLinks`) holds unordered pairs of entity ids, adjacency kept sorted; saved, hashed, part of replay. At least one end is a **link point** — a pillar (pole, pylon) or the Hearth, content `pole: { range, slots }` — and the other a link point or any building that makes, uses or stores power (`linkable`: `kw`, `idleKw`, `gen`, `accumulator`, `pole`). **Reach** is measured footprint edge to footprint edge (`gap2`, integer squared distances) and is the longer range of the two ends (either end reaching is enough): pole 6 tiles / 8 slots, pylon (2×2, D75) 40 (+ `mods.pylonLink`, 16 with xp4) / 4, the Hearth 8 / 6. Ordinary buildings have no slot limit (a building wired to two pillars joins their grids). `linkCheck(a, b, …, level)` gives the first refusal: `missing`, `self`, `not_linkable`, `needs_pillar`, `already`, `range`, `steep`, `blocked`, `slots` (`a` full), `slots_other` (`b` full). **The land** (D45, D78; `level` is the land's tile level, `landOf` in systems/power.ts): a wire is as tolerant as its taller end (`wireClimb`, content `pole.climb`: pole and Hearth 1, pylon 3, any other building 0). `steep` (`wireSteep`): its two ends' levels more than that apart. `blocked` (`wireObstacle`): a tile it passes over rises above it — the wire runs from footprint centre to footprint centre, `wireClimb` levels above the straight line joining the two ends' grounds, sampled every quarter tile along the longer axis in integers, the footprints' own tiles skipped; it returns the first such tile from `a`, which the Link tool marks. So two poles on the floor wire across a one-terrace ridge, not a two-terrace one; two pylons on the floor clear a three-terrace hill, never a mountain; a pylon wires to another three terraces up. The rule is read when a wire is strung (by the player, a new pillar's reach-out, a replacement): wires already strung stay when the land changes under them.
- **Commands** ([systems/power.ts](../src/sim/systems/power.ts)): `link(a, b)` / `unlink(a, b)` (logged, replayed; `canLink` previews). A placement (`Sim.place`) carries the wires of what it replaces over to the new building where they still fit — link points first, then buildings, in id order; the rest are cut (a pylon over a pole keeps 4 of its wires) — then a **new pillar** with no wire wires itself to the nearest pillar or Hearth in reach with a free slot (smallest gap, then lowest id; none in reach: no wire). Generators and consumers are wired by the player. `Sim.remove` cuts the removed building's wires; `ensureCompiled` also prunes wires to entities gone by any other path.
- **Grids** ([network.ts](../src/sim/power/network.ts) `buildGrids`, called by the compiler): the connected components of the wires (union-find over member indices) among the **members** — built, awake, linkable entities; a wire to a construction site or a dormant building carries nothing until it stands. Every link point heads a grid (alone until wired); an ordinary building belongs to one only through a wire, else `node.net = -1` (status `Unpowered`). Grid ids follow the smallest link-point id of each component; `Net.poles` lists them; `graph.netOfEnt` covers every member (pillars included).
- **Producers** (per grid, sorted by id): the Hearth's built-in **solar panel** (`BAL.hearthSolarKw[level − 1]` = 90 kW at full sun at level 1, then 130, 180, 240, × `solarOut × genOut` like a solar panel, so it follows `SOLAR‰`: nothing at night, like every panel) plus its **isotope core** (`Producer.base` = `BAL.hearthCoreKw[level − 1]` = 210 kW at level 1, then 300, 420, 560, × `genOut`, day and night: about 70 % of the Core's noon power, D101; a base founded at night is never dead; `producerOf` reads the Core's level, `CompileInput.hearthLevel`, so raising it recompiles both), wind turbines (D95: a rating `gen.kw × windOut × genOut` reached on the turbine's curve `gen.wind`, at the local wind = the weather's wind × the turbine's exposure for the day's bearing, `Producer.local` holding its three; [Wind](#wind)), solar panels (× sun ‰), geothermal (constant). Accumulators: capacity `kwMin·600 × accCap`, rate `kw × accRate`.
- **The weather and the generators' output** are small named functions so a new input is added in one place (temperature next): `weatherAt(absEpoch, worldSeed, mods)` → `Weather { sun, gust, wind, veer }`, `weatherKey` (`sun:wind:veer`: the catch-up re-solves when it changes), `solarKw`, `windKw(kw, curve, local, w)`, `turbineWind(local, w)`, `producerKw(p, w)`, `renewableKw(net, w)`; `producerOf(e, def, grid, mods, worldSeed)` builds a building's `Producer` (the compile and the inspector's grid module read the same) and `windSite(grid, worldSeed, e)` a turbine's exposure for the day's three bearings (D95: world/wind.ts `turbineSite`); on the demand side `gridDemand(nodes)` and `fuelKw(nodes)` (systems/power.ts). Online and offline call the same functions.
- **Solve** ([solve.ts](../src/sim/power/solve.ts), online in [systems/power.ts](../src/sim/systems/power.ts)): per grid, demand `D` = Σ `node.demand()` (from **last tick's** statuses), renewables `R`, fuelled generator capacity `F`. Merit order: renewables → fuel generators (`dispatch = ⌈gen·1000/F⌉` rounded up to 25 ‰, `DISPATCH_STEP`, D138; fuel burnt ∝ dispatch) → accumulators (discharge ≤ rate). Satisfaction `sat = served/D` floored to 25 ‰; every consumer of the grid runs at `speed × sat`. A surplus charges the accumulators (`spreadAcc`, in id order). The catch-up solves power per regime instead ([offline.md](offline.md)).

## Climate

[world/climate.ts](../src/sim/world/climate.ts) gives the cirque desert's air temperature in **deci-°C** (215 = 21.5 °C): a pure integer function of the absolute world tick (`absEpoch · EPOCH + tick in the epoch`, i.e. `foundingEpoch · EPOCH + sim.tick`) and `WORLD_SEED`, like the sun and the gusts — the same for every base, online and offline, never saved.

- **The day** (`BAL.climate.curve`, `baseTemp`): 36 values, one at the start of each day epoch, interpolated tick by tick. The desert cools fast after sunset (19.6 °C at 20:00) and slowly through the night, is coldest at the end of it (4.0 °C at 04:00, day epoch 12), warms quickly once the sun is up and peaks a little after noon (37.0 °C at 14:00, day epoch 27).
- **Each day's weather**: a world day (`absEpoch / 36`) draws an offset in [−1000, 1000] ‰ from `hash32(worldSeed, day, salt)`; offsets are blended linearly from one day start to the next, so the temperature never jumps. The offset is worth `dayVarLow` (±2 °C) at the curve's coldest and `dayVarPeak` (±3 °C) at its hottest: nights run 2–6 °C, afternoons 34–40 °C.
- **Exactness** (`temperatureAt`): curve, blend and worth make one exact fraction (every term below 2^53) floored once, so the value moves by at most 1 deci-°C per tick, across epochs and days.
- **Power reads one value per epoch** (`epochTemp(absEpoch)`, the value at the epoch's middle tick), like the sun, so a whole epoch stays one regime for the catch-up.
- **Effects, prepared, not wired yet** (they will join `weatherAt`): `solarHeatPermille(t)` — solar panels and the Heart's array lose `solarLoss` = 4 ‰ per °C above `solarRef` = 25 °C (940 ‰ at 40 °C); `heaterKw(t)` — **survival heaters**: nobody lives at the base, but its electronics (the Heart, the accumulators' cells) must stay above a cold limit, like a planetary rover's, and draw `heaterKwPerDeg` = 8 kW per °C below `heaterBelow` = 15 °C (104 kW on a 2 °C night).
- The HUD shows the temperature beside the clock ([ui.md](ui.md)); the renderer's day / night look reads the same world clock ([render.md](render.md)). Tests: [climate.test.ts](../src/sim/world/climate.test.ts) (determinism, the desert range, ±1 per tick across epochs and days, the daily shape, the effects).

## Wind

[world/wind.ts](../src/sim/world/wind.ts) (D95; owner, 2026-10-16: « réellement une simulation du vent… plus de vent au sommet des collines, ou dans les grands espaces sans obstacles »). A turbine catches **the weather's wind × its place's exposure**, both pure integer functions — of the world clock and `WORLD_SEED`, and of the land — the same for every base, online and offline, never saved. Numbers in `BAL.wind`.

- **The time** (`windAt(absEpoch, worldSeed, gustVariance)`, ‰ of the reference wind over open flat ground): the day's curve (`curve`, 36 values: the Sough rises at dusk and blows through the night, ≈ 1 270 at midnight, lulls in the late morning, ≈ 760) × the day's mood (`moodLow`…`moodHigh`, 650–1 350 ‰, `hash32(worldSeed, day, salt)`, blended linearly from this day's start to the next one's: `calmAt`) × the gust window (`gustPermille`, the 10-epoch windows above), rounded to `step` (50 ‰): ≈ 40 distinct winds a week.
- **The bearing**: where the wind comes from, in the trig tables' 64 steps (0 east, 16 south): `prevailing(worldSeed)`, one draw for the world; on a world day (`veerOf`) the prevailing bearing (60 % of days) or `veer` (4) steps either side (20 % each). `Weather.veer` (0, 1, 2) picks which of a turbine's three exposures applies (`bearingOf`).
- **The place** (`WindField`, one per `TerrainSource`: `windField(source)`): a tile's **exposure** ‰ for a bearing, from the land's levels **as the world made them** — the field refills each chunk from the source into its own cache, so a dig never moves the wind and the field is the same after a reload; a level counts the same on every map (D94's massifs need nothing of their own). The readings, every one in tiles and levels (`read`), from a 96×96 patch round the chunk (`PAD` 32) with box sums and ray offset tables (one add a step):
  - **height**: the tile's level against the mean of its 13×13 box (`relRadius` 6): `relGain` 340 ‰ a level above, `relGainLow` 120 a level below; plus `levelGain` 40 a level;
  - **openness**: the share of the upwind fan (the bearing in full, `side` 6 steps either way at half) clear of higher ground over the `fetch` (32 tiles): up to `openGain` 380;
  - **basin**: the share of eight bearings meeting higher ground within the fetch: up to −`basinGain` 480 (the cirque's floor);
  - **funnel**: the way upwind clear 16 tiles and higher ground within 8 on both flanks: a pass, +`funnel` 300 — its walls then shelter nothing and close no fan, and neither the bowl nor standing low counts;
  - **shelter**: the steepest higher ground upwind, levels per tile (Winstral's shelter index; the side rays at half): `shelterGain` 1.8 × it, at most `shelterMax` 850 ‰, taken off the sum: `e = (base 820 + the rest) × (1000 − shelter) / 1000`, clamped to `min` 60…`max` 2 600.
  Buildings shelter nothing. Exposures are kept per chunk and bearing (`Uint16Array`), read tile by tile on demand (`at`) or whole for the view (`chunk`); `explain` gives every reading (the inspector's reasons).
- **A turbine** (`turbineSite`, through `windSite`): the mean exposure of its footprint, for each of the day's three bearings; on a tile dug or filled since (its level is not the world's) the readings are taken at its real level. Its **curve** (content `gen.wind: { cutIn, rated, cutOut? }`, ‰ of the reference wind; `curveShare`): nothing below cut-in, the cube of the wind from cut-in to rated (`(v³ − a³)/(r³ − a³)`), all of its rating above, nothing from cut-out (a storm) — none on the pitch-control turbine. `meanExposure` weighs the three bearings by their days.
- **Exactness**: the producers' exposures are fixed at compile (the turbine's foot cannot change under it); the time's wind changes only at epoch boundaries. Tests: [wind.test.ts](../src/sim/world/wind.test.ts) (the time, the curve, a wall's lee, a bowl, a pass, digs, the real maps against the old numbers, online ≈ offline).

## The Gleaners (D103)

A small desert people that comes out at night and takes a few shiny things off the ribbons it reaches ([lore.md](lore.md#the-gleaners); the numbers in `BAL.gleaners`, their taste in [content/gleaners.ts](../src/content/gleaners.ts)). Nothing is commanded: the theft is a rule of the ribbons (its one state, a raid's held posts, hashed with the chain), the rest statistics.

- **Where** ([world/reach.ts](../src/sim/world/reach.ts) `gleanLand`, run by `compileGraph` into `Graph.glean`): they walk free floor (`isFloor`, nothing standing on it) at level 0, past the Crown's foot (`TerrainSource.crownOuter`; a source without it, the tests' `FlatSource`, has no Gleaners). A pocket of such floor of at most `pocket` (1 500) tiles, closed in by rock, water, gaps, buildings, ribbons or fences, is out of their reach; anything larger is the open desert. A bounded flood from each floor tile beside a ribbon, memoised by tile (the verdict is the pocket's, whatever the flood's order: deterministic), on typed scratch arrays stamped per call. A camouflaged ribbon (`BuildingDef.camo`, D109; `buried`) is no wall to them: buried under the sand, its tile is floor they walk over, so a buried line closes no pocket. A ribbon tile (not a conduit) beside reachable floor is **exposed**; one under glass (`BuildingDef.glazed`) beside it, a buried one anywhere beside floor (they never see it), or one beside a closed pocket, is **guarded**. For the views, `glass` (glazed tiles beside reachable floor) and `prowl` (reachable floor beside a fence). Rebuilt at every compile (≈ 0.75 ms on the late base, 92 compiles of its build: 70 ms).
- **When**: the dark hours, `gleanNight(absEpoch)` = `SOLAR` ≤ `darkSun` (0) — a world-clock input that changes only at a minute boundary, like the sun. Online `gleanBefore` sets `ctx.glean` before the phases.
- **How**: group raids (D112; owner, 2026-10-17: « ils doivent venir en groupe, aller vers un tapis où il y a des objets en mouvement, et réellement les récupérer », 2026-10-18: « il faut qu'ils aillent nécessairement vers un tapis où il y a des items, et si possible en priorité les items qui valent cher … Ils ne doivent pas rester là deux minutes à regarder le tapis »; [flow/chain.ts](../src/sim/flow/chain.ts) `planRaid`, `raidStep`). The compile gives each item ribbon chain its exposed cells (`gleanCells`) and a draw (`gleanSeed`, a hash of the world seed and its head tile), and `planRaid` lays out its raid slot, one a `GLEAN_WINDOW` (`window`: 2 400 ticks on the world's clock): `raidPosts`, a run of `raidSize` exposed cells in a row round a hashed centre (one a cell up to `group.min` 3, then one more each `group.per` 8 cells, at most `group.max` 5; never the head), and `raidAt`, the slot in the window (the raid's `RAID_TICKS` = `arrive` + `take` = 220 ticks fit inside). **The call**: at night, on the line's first step within `callBy` (40) ticks of its slot, the raid is called (`raidBandNow`): by the most valuable thing to their taste on the line up to its last post (what will still ride past them; centi-◉ from the items' reference value, `gleanValue`), `raidBandFor` says how many come — `band.base` 2, one more at each of `band.more` 5 / 12 / 40 ◉, no more than its posts — and none for a line that carries nothing for them (no window draw: a night group keeps its period). **The takes**: the band crawls in for `arrive` (100) ticks; then for at most `take` (120: twelve seconds) ticks, on each step and **before** the items move, called post `p` — open from `arrive + p × stagger` (8) — looks at the item on its cell and takes it if it is to its taste (a taste under 1 000 ‰: a hash roll): the whole item leaves the line, counted in `ctx.gleaned` (online `Sim.gleaned`) and logged for the views (`ctx.gleanLog`, online only), and the post holds it. `raidHeld` (hashed, snapshotted, saved as the chain's optional `raid`) keeps the posts that have taken (bits 0–7), the call (bit 8) and the band (bits 9–11; `raidCalled`, `raidBandOf`); cleared on the line's first step after the raid. The rule is one function, `raidTakes(seed, p, k, item)`; `Chain.raidWillTake(p, tick, night)` reads it ahead on the line as it stands before that tick (the item on the post's cell, the step, the post called and still empty-handed) — what the views reach for; the head is never a post, its item being free to leave on that very step. So a raid goes only to a line carrying something for them, with more of them for shinier things, takes at most one item a post within seconds of arriving, and is over twelve seconds after; the item taken is the one the player saw sitting on that cell. The views know every raid slot ahead (`raidPosts`, `raidAt`) and read its call (`raidHeld`); tests check that an empty or ore-only line is never raided and a line of circuits calls a bigger band than one of glass, that the first take comes within four seconds of arriving and every take inside the short window, every theft on a called post after its turn, one a post, and tick by tick that the takes `raidWillTake` foresees are the takes made, each the token that was on the post's cell.
- **Offline**: a flow rule, so the catch-up runs it in its warm-ups: the night is a regime condition ([offline.md](offline.md#the-gleaners-d103)).
- **The tally** ([systems/gleaners.ts](../src/sim/systems/gleaners.ts)): `Sim.gleaned` per item (statistics: saved, not hashed); `gleanEpoch` at each minute boundary (online after the expertises, the catch-up at the same place): at nightfall `dusk` = the count and the marks cleared, at dawn `dawn` = the count and a `gleaners` event `dawn` with what the night took (`takenSince`: the night so far, or by day the last one; `Sim.gleanedTonight()`); their first take of a night pushes `night` (online at once in `gleanAfter`, in a catch-up at the boundary). `marks` (tile → milli-items since dusk): each theft online, a group's takings offline (`markTaken`, at its most exposed chain). `recent`: the last thefts (tile, item, tick), online, for the views.
- **Conservation**: Δheld = Δproduced − Δconsumed − Δgleaned (`gleaners.test.ts`).
- Tests: [gleaners.test.ts](../src/sim/gleaners.test.ts) — reach (the cirque never, a rock pocket and a fence closing a line in, a gap reopening it, no Crown no Gleaners), night only, taste (ore left), the share taken on a full line, the raids (every take on a post, in its raid, after its turn, one a post; the raid's size), glass, the fence drag, dusk and dawn and the day's tally, save → load → go on, replay of a night's commands, online ≈ offline over two nights bit for bit (stocks, stores, takings, the morning reports, `hash()`).

## CrierBelt's restart reserve (D36)

A base can always rebuild its production. The Heart only takes (D34) and plans are paid for good (D33), so without a guard a new base could spend on plans the plates, glass and copper wire its production is made of, and be left with nothing that makes them and nothing to build it with. Content says what rebuilding takes ([content.md](content.md#the-restart-reserve--bootstrap-d36)): `BOOTSTRAP`, the stages that rebuild production from the bare land, and `reserveNeed`, what they cost from the Heart's stock (59 plates, 6 glass, 4 copper wire at founding). [systems/reserve.ts](../src/sim/systems/reserve.ts) keeps one invariant, for every reserved item *i*:

```
assets(i) = stock(i) + Σ cost(i) of every building standing   ≥   reserve(i)
```

- **Definitions.** *Essential* items are what the bootstrap makes (`ESSENTIAL`: iron plates, glass, copper wire); every plan and the starting kit are paid in them (validated). A base is *recoverable* when it can take every building down (removal refunds a building's cost in full, plus its buffers and stores) and then build the bootstrap's stages in order: each stage from the Heart's stock, except what an earlier stage makes, which it can wait for. The invariant is exactly what that needs: after taking everything down, the stock is at least `assets`.
- **Why it holds.** At founding: `validateContent` checks that the starting stock covers the reserve, with the whole first ring of plans bought. Placing and removing never change `assets`: the stock pays what the building's cost adds, a replacement refunds what it replaces first, a removal gives the cost back. Production into the Heart, quest rewards, refunds and deliveries only raise it, and the Heart feeds nothing (D34). What lowers it for good is spending that no removal gives back: a plan. `planBlock` refuses a plan when `reserveShort(cost, id)` finds an item short. A plan whose whole cost is in the reserve (the smelter's) never trips the guard: its share of the reserve goes with its cost. The wire mill's can: its plates are not kept, since stage 1 makes them.
- **Why it is enough.** From any state that holds the invariant, a base can rebuild every stage, and with plates, glass and copper wire flowing it can make everything else. `validateContent` checks this from content (`reserveProblems`). [reserve.test.ts](../src/sim/systems/reserve.test.ts) runs it on the real cirque (a base holding exactly the reserve, for 24 players, at noon and at midnight on the Heart's core alone) and fuzzes it (random plans, placements, ribbons, removals, wires, recipes, smelter lines, a trade post's export streams and time, for spending and for building players: the invariant at every step, then a full rebuild from the tightest and the last state, and a replay).
- **No state.** The reserve is derived from content and `done`, the assets from the stock and the entities: no command, nothing saved or hashed, nothing for the catch-up. A refused plan logs nothing, so a replay meets the same refusals. `buildingRefunds` is cached per entity table (`ents.sorted()` is the same array until an add or a remove). The facade has `reserveShort(cost, plan?)` and `reserveFloor(item)` (what the stock itself must keep: the reserve less what the buildings cover, which the tree's header shows).
- **Trade posts** never sell the Heart's stock (D64: a post sells only what its entrance brings), so they cannot break it.
- **The other permanent spending** from the Heart's stock goes through the same check (`reserveShort`, refusal `reserve`): a spot sale from the Heart's stock (`spotTrade`, the Crier's « Vendre »), a Heart level (`upgradeHearth`), and the remote surcharge of a ribbon or conduit off the claim's land (`canPlace`: the part above `costOf`, which no removal gives back). Land is paid in ocelles, never from the stock (D60), so it cannot touch the reserve. Still open: streams keep `BAL.tradeHearthReserve` only; `max(tradeHearthReserve, reserveFloor(item))` would keep them safe if the numbers change.
- **A spot sale from the Heart's stock pays CrierBelt `BAL.coreSellFee`** (400 ‰, D126; `spotFee` in systems/trade.ts), twice a post's `tradeFee` on what its entrance sells: goods are meant to reach the post by ribbon. Spot purchases pay the post's fee.

## Expertises (D93)

[systems/expertise.ts](../src/sim/systems/expertise.ts), content in [expertises.ts](../src/content/expertises.ts) ([content.md](content.md#expertises-d93)); `Sim.expertise` (`ExpertiseState`): `ranks` (by id, 1–3), `slots` (`BAL.expertise.slots`: `{ id, at, on }` or null), `reorients`, `streak` and `best` (the Core's grid). - **The minute's boundary** (`expertiseEpoch(satOf)`, last of the boundary's calls: online after `tokenEpoch` with `ctx.sat`, in the catch-up at the same place with the segment's grid modes): the Core's grid served `gridPermille` at the minute's end → `streak + 1` (and `best`), else 0; each expertise's feat (`featValue`) against its thresholds: a rank rises, never falls — announced by an `expertise` event (`what: 'earned'`) once the Core mounts the Archive (`archiveOpen`: level `BAL.expertise.level`), in silence before; a slot whose `at` (a world minute) has come is put in place (`on`, `what: 'ready'`). When an expertise in place changed rank or came into place it calls `rebuildDerived` and returns true: online the graph recompiles at the next tick, the catch-up materializes its groups and recompiles there, as for a lease lapsing.
- **The feats** read what the colony keeps anyway: `produced` by item kind (`mined`: ore, fuel, rubble, unique; `made`: material, part), the research nodes learnt (`research`, D122: data made until then), the Crier's lifetime `trade.earned` / `trade.spent`, `tokenStats.totalUsed`, `ents.defCounts()` (`capacity`: Σ `gen.kw`; `machines`; `ribbons`), `land.bought.size`, `grid.terrainDiff.size`; plus `best` for `grid`. Each is exact at a boundary whenever the regimes are (the catch-up's `produced` follows them; the rest changes by commands or at boundaries).
- **Commands** (logged, replayed): `chooseExpertise(slot, id)` — `{ k: 'expertise' }`, into an empty slot, in place at once, free; `reorient(slot, id)` — `{ k: 'reorient' }`, a filled slot: `reorientCost()` (`reorientCents` × `reorientGrowth` ‰ for each one before) leaves the purse, the old one goes at once (its effect with it), the new one is `on` at the first boundary `gapMinutes` (24 h) later. Refusals (`chooseBlock`, `reorientBlock`): `unknown`, `locked` (no Archive yet), `slot`, `full` (choose into a filled slot), `empty` (reorient an empty one), `unearned`, `held` (already in a slot), `funds`.
- **Effects**: `heldExpertises` (the slots `on`, at their rank) go into `computeMods(done, held)`. Production: `genOut` (every generator and the Core's panel), `drillRate` (new: drills, face cutters, sand collectors — their rate and their deposit's cap; `drillRate()` in compile.ts), `craftSpeed`, `tokenRate` (D122: the Core's compute, `coreComputeRate`, and every server and autonomous lab's `tpm` in `graph.servers`; `dataRate` until then), `deliveryDelay`, `landPrice` (and so the rent). Research: `fam[family].time / tokens / resources`, read by `effectiveCost` (time takes off the tokens since D118).
- **Tier 2** (`tierTwoOpen`): both slots filled (a réorientation keeps its slot filled), or a node of Tier 2 or beyond already held by an older claim (save v11 also gives it the Synthesis) — read off the frozen `TIER2_UP_V10` (content/retired.ts), and only a node still of Tier 2 or beyond today, so a node the tree moved later (the Sifter and the Standing Orders up to Tier 2, the Fast Drill down to Tier 1, D107) opens nothing.
- Tests: [expertise.test.ts](../src/sim/systems/expertise.test.ts) — the table and the arc, feats and ranks at the boundary, choose / reorient / the price / the gap, a drill's rate, a family's research, saves and the commands' replay, and online ≈ offline bit for bit with a rank rising and a slot coming into place during a two-hour absence.

## Space: digs, fills

[space.ts](../src/sim/space.ts) holds the pure rules; [systems/landscape.ts](../src/sim/systems/landscape.ts) applies them.

- **Marks**: `designate` marks rock (`dig`) or shallow/deep/salt tiles (`fill`) in marking order (saved). A rock tile holding a face ore (D87: the quartz vein's face) takes no mark and is never quarried: the vein stays a wall for the face cutters.
- **Jobs**: `assignJobs` gives each idle, built, non-dormant excavator (reach 3 tiles from its footprint's edge, D54) or backfiller (reach 8) the nearest workable mark (doubled-centre d², ties to the earliest mark); one job per machine. An excavator with no workable mark in reach and room in its store **quarries** (owner, 2026-10-14: the stone must not run out, « pour l'instant »): a job on the nearest rock it reaches and can dig (`quarryTile`, `Job.quarry`), its stone like a dig's, the rock left as it is — when it ends the next starts; a mark that becomes workable takes the machine over at once. It runs when `jobsDirty` is set and, online, at every epoch boundary. A job's duration comes from the hardness (`BAL.digMinutes`, `digSpeed`, basalt bits; H3/H4 need `digH3`/`digH4`) or `fillSeconds`; its stone from `digRubble`/`fillCost` (deep water needs `deepFill`).
- **Pattern**: a job has `el` and `moved`; `advanceJob` moves stone under the **same allowance** as research (`⌊mu·el/end⌋`) into the excavator's store (digging pauses while the store is at its cap) or out of the backfiller's store (only what is there). On completion the tile turns to `Dug`/`Filled` (the land it lies on does not change hands; its parcel's price follows its new ground), a dug tile gives up embedded ore to the Hearth (`embedded`, a deterministic draw per tile), and nearby pumps force a recompile.

These long processes are not flow nodes: they hang off **reservoir nodes** (excavator, backfiller; research labs until D122), which are group boundaries for the catch-up. That is the **pseudo-flow + allowance** pattern: the catch-up sees each process as a known in/out-flow of a reservoir and advances it with the same function over whole segments ([offline.md](offline.md)).

## Trade: the trade posts and the Crier

The colony trades with the simulated world market of [src/market](../src/market/README.md) through its **trade posts** (kind `trade`, the Comptoir). Rules in [trade.ts](../src/sim/trade.ts) (types, levels, slots, throughput, delays, hysteresis), applied by [systems/trade.ts](../src/sim/systems/trade.ts):

- **State** (`sim.trade`, saved and hashed): each post's **cargo** (`cargo`: the items it bought and holds or awaits — on order, on their way, or still in its container — sorted; D64), standing import orders (`orders`: item, whole items/min, max price in centi-◉, hysteresis flag; `orderSlots` = 2, 6 with Standing Orders), goods in flight (`inflight`: arrival tick, always a boundary, and what each cost — for the post's panel, not hashed), the epoch's `sold` / `bought` accumulators, a rolling `ledger` (2 000 lines), per-post `activity`, lifetime `earned` / `spent` / `soldTotal` / `boughtTotal`, and `starterTaken` (the starter contract was signed, below). `sim.market` is the `MarketService` (its own save rides in the colony save) and `sim.prices` this epoch's bid / ask table.
- **The post**: 5×5, the pad in the middle and a one-tile flame apron round it (its own ground: nothing else stands there), crossed by its two built-in flame-proof ribbons: one entrance (port 0) and one exit (port 1), on opposite sides (D62). It compiles to **two** `ReservoirNode`s (D131), each with its own store (two minutes of throughput): the post's node (`nodeByEnt`; its power, its exit, port 1) holds its **container** (`sim.stores`), its cargo, which the exit hands out in turn (`cycle`, from `importItems`); its **entrance**'s node (`Graph.postIn`, the in-port's) holds what the entrance brought (`sim.selling`, saved, hashed, refunded with the post), every tradable item — its cargo's too (`exportAccepts`) — to sell. Two reservoirs, so the catch-up keys them apart: a purchase is never sold, a sale never leaves by the exit. Until D131 one store held both and the entrance refused the cargo: a line bringing an item the post imported backed up at the entrance (owner, 2026-10-18: « le tradepost est bloqué… tant pis pour le joueur »). An order or a spot purchase makes its item cargo (`addCargo`: the exit hands it out; the entrance still takes it, to sell); an item leaves the cargo at a boundary once no order wants it, none is on its way and not one is left in the container (`pruneCargo`) — the graph is then recompiled, online and in the catch-up alike (`tradeEpoch` returns it). Ribbons fill it during the minute; a sorter downstream splits the exit's mix.
- **At each epoch boundary** (`tradeEpoch`, online at the end of `step`, offline in the catch-up loop — the same call): (1) shipments due land in their post's **container** (its store, D43) while it has room — at most one lift's load, `containerMu` = the post's throughput (120 / 240 / 480 items) of purchases waiting (`waitingMu`: the store's cargo items); one that does not fit waits in the air, and so do the post's next ones; a post gone or dormant: into the Hearth. Purchases leave the container only by its exit, linked to a ribbon or a building (`handsOut`); a post against the Hearth no longer delivers into it; (2) per post, within `throughputMu(level)` (120 / 240 / 480 items/min, imports and exports together): active orders buy first — only while its exit is linked, and none while the container is full (`orderHold`: `price`, `outlet`, `full`; nothing is spent on goods that could not come out) (`orderTrades` hysteresis: pause above the max, resume 3 % below it; whole items the ocelles cover; delivery after `deliveryTicks` = lift delay + the nearest supplier's delay × `deliveryDelay` ‰), then it sells, whole items in item order, everything its entrance brought (its entrance's store, `sellingOf`, its cargo's items too since D131 — the player may buy and sell the same item, and pays both) at the epoch's bid minus CrierBelt's fifth (`mods.tradeFee`, 200 ‰, D64); (3) the cargo handed out in full leaves it (`pruneCargo`); (4) `market.settle({ epoch: absEpoch − 1, sold, bought })` → `sim.prices`; (5) events: `trade` (per post, for the cargo drone), `delivered`, `gazette`.
- **Spot trades** (`spotTrade`): sold from the Hearth, or bought into the first post whose exit is linked (refused `no_outlet`; more than a container's load: `container`; a full container: `full`), priced and **booked** on the market at once (`book`: splitting a sale gains nothing), fee on top; a purchase is delivered after the post's delays. `quoteSpot` is the pure preview.
- **The starter contract** (D101): CrierBelt's one-time offer for a new base's first purchase, `BAL.starter` — copper ore at a fixed 2 ◉ an item (no fee: half the ore's market value), 40 in all, 10 a minute. `takeStarter(post)` (the `starter` command) opens it as a standing order with `starter` (the mu still to buy), sets `starterTaken` and makes the ore cargo, as `addImport` does; refused `no_post`, `taken` (once per base, ever: a cancel does not give it back) or `slots` (it takes an order slot). In `tradeEpoch` (`starterBuys`) it is always active (no hysteresis) but held like any order (`orderHold`: an exit linked to nothing, a full container — so the player learns to link the exit to where the ore is used); it buys min(its rate, what is left, the throughput, what the purse pays at the fixed price), books `spent`, `boughtTotal` and an `import` ledger line but never `bought`, so the market never sees it and no price moves; each lot lands `delayMin` = 1 minute later, on a boundary, from `colony` −1 (CrierBelt itself; the Crier's « En route » names it). **Bought in full, it stops** (owner, 2026-10-16: the tutorial's order bought on for hours, unseen): the order stands with `starter` 0, held 'fulfilled' (`orderHold`), buying nothing at any price, its goods cargo while it stands. The player either continues it at the market's price — `continueStarter(id)`, the logged `starterGo` command: `starter` dropped, an ordinary order from then on, its cap the ask and `BAL.starter.continueOver` (110 %) — or stops it (`cancelImport`, the notice's default). It runs in the same `tradeEpoch` online and offline. Tests: trade.test.ts (the outlet hold, 10 a minute and 40 in all, the delay, the stop at the cap and nothing bought after, « Continuer » at the market's price and its replay, « Arrêter », once per base, the market untouched, replay, save → load, the absence). The Crier's post card offers it until it is signed (`[data-starter]`, `[data-starter-take="<post>"]`); its order's row carries « Contrat de départ · prix fixe », its price and what is left as a bar (`[data-order-starter]`), and once fulfilled « Continuer au prix du marché » / « Arrêter » (`[data-starter-go]`, `[data-starter-stop]`); a notice says it too (ui/contract-notice.ts).
- **What an order costs** (D101): `orderUnitCents(sim, o)` (one item at the next lift: the ask with the fee and express freight's share, or the contract's fixed price) and `orderCentsPerMin(sim, o)` (its rate at that price — the contract's as many as it has left —, or 0 while it holds). Every row of the Crier shows it, « −37 ◉/min » in coral (`[data-order-cost]`), or « — » with why it holds; the purse's breakdown (D102) can read the same number.
- **Commands**: `importOrder`, `cancelOrder`, `starter`, `starterGo`, `spot`, `autoSell` (D127, [below](#auto-sell-d127)), `acceptDelivery` (D153, [below](#crierbelts-delivery-orders-d153)) (`Command` variants, logged, replayed; there is nothing to set on a post's entrance, D64). A second post needs « Second comptoir » (`post2`, Commerce's Tier 1 since D107; `tradeL2` still counts for claims from before), a third the Crier House (`maxPosts`, refusal `limit`). Removing a post drops its cargo and orders; goods in flight then land in the Hearth.
- **Exactness**: everything is integer at the sim boundary (centi-◉, mu); the market computes in floats but is deterministic. "trading during an absence matches ticking" ([trade.test.ts](../src/sim/trade.test.ts)) checks the same ocelles, goods and state hash.

### Auto-sell (D127)

Commerce's Tier 2 node `cm_autosell` « Vente automatique » (flag `autoSell`; owner, 2026-10-18: « une amélioration de l'arbre permette de faire de la vente automatique des items qui arrivent en surplus dans le cœur (+10 000) »). Rules in [systems/trade.ts](../src/sim/systems/trade.ts):

- **The setting** (`sim.trade.autoSell`: item → threshold in whole items; saved in the trade section, optional, and hashed when not empty): `setAutoSell(item, at)`, the logged `{ k: 'autoSell', item, at }` — on or moved at `at` (`BAL.autoSell.min`, 100, up to the Core's cap, `autoSellMax`; the UI's default `BAL.autoSell.at`, 10 000, at most the cap), off with `null`. Refused without the research, for an item the Crier does not take (`tradable`: a fluid, no depth), out of bounds, or when nothing changes. It acts only while `autoSell` is in the Mods; a setting left without it stays, inert.
- **The Core's cap for it** (D163): the Core keeps its usual cap for an auto-sold item (until D163 `autoSellCaps` lifted it, and an item nobody bought piled up past the cap without end). The highest threshold is `BAL.autoSell.maxShare` (900 ‰) of the cap (`autoSellMax`), and what the Core keeps (`autoSellKeep`) never exceeds it, so a minute's arrivals fit under the cap. An item with no buyer (a bid of nothing) is not sold: it fills to the cap and backs its line up, seen, and the Stock says « Personne n'en achète ».
- **The sale** (`autoSales`, inside `tradeEpoch`, after the posts' own sales, before the cargo and the settlement): each switched-on item in item order, its whole items beyond what the Core keeps (`autoSellKeep`) at the epoch's bid less `spotFee`'s sale (`BAL.coreSellFee`, 40 %, D126); booked like a post's sales — `credits`, `earned`, `t.sold` (the market sees the supply at its settlement), `soldTotal` — and on the ledger as an `autoSell` line from the Core (`post` = `hearthId`), which `line()` books in the purse's journal as `autoSell` (recurring). The posts' floor prices are theirs: the Core's surplus goes at the bid. No trade post is needed.
- **What it keeps** (`autoSellKeep`): the threshold, never above the Core's cap (beyond it the Core would have refused the item: selling it is the point); raised to the largest cost of the item in an open plan (prerequisites met, not taken) or a queued research's resources (`labQueue`, D122) — a sale never makes the base short of what the Stock's `shortages` would flag —; never below CrierBelt's restart reserve (`reserveFloor`, D36). An item with no bid waits.
- **Offline**: nothing of its own. Within a minute an auto-sold item is an ordinary level between its bounds, the regimes carry it exactly, and the boundary's `tradeEpoch` sells as online.
- Tests: [autosell.test.ts](../src/sim/autosell.test.ts) — the command's bounds, refusals and replay; the sale at the bid less 40 %, on the ledger and the journal; a full Core whose drill blocks against one that auto-sells and runs on, the cap back when switched off; what it keeps (an open plan, a queued research, the cap); saves and an older save; online ≈ offline over two hours, bit for bit.

### CrierBelt's delivery orders (D153)

« Commandes de la Criée »: timed orders for the base's own goods, paid above the market — a base's goals past the tutorial. Rules in [systems/orders.ts](../src/sim/systems/orders.ts), the goods and kits in [content/orders.ts](../src/content/orders.ts) (`ORDER_GOODS`, `ORDER_KITS`), every number in `BAL.orders` ([content.md](content.md#bal)).

- **State** (`sim.deliveries`, saved by item id, hashed but for the history): the orders standing (`open`, in the order they were posted: item, `n` asked and `got` delivered — whole items —, `cents` an item, `posted`, `offerEnd`, `windowMin`, `due` — the epoch its window closes once accepted, −1 while offered —, `event`, `first`, `reward`), `nextId`, `firstPosted`; the last closed (`past`, `BAL.orders.past`) and the lifetime `filled` / `missed` / `paid`, statistics.
- **Posted** at a boundary (`deliveryEpoch`, inside `tradeEpoch` after every post's turn): once the tutorial is over or skipped (`tutorialOver`), the first at the next boundary — `BAL.orders.first`, 120 quartz in 40 minutes (D146's problem 7: the Core piles quartz up and nothing tells the player it sells) —, then one every `everyMin` (15) world minutes (`absEpoch % everyMin`) while fewer than `open` (3) stand. Drawn by `drawOrder` from `hash32(worldSeed ^ salt, playerSeed, epoch, k)` — one lane per choice — among `orderGoods`: the goods the base can make now (`canMake`: a recipe making it unlocked with a machine that runs it, or for a raw good the nodes in its `needs`), at its Core's level (`level`), with a bid, none another standing order asks for; weighted by `w`. Its size: the level's `worth` (◉ at the item's reference value), drawn in `spread`, `roundItems`; its price: the bid then plus `premium` (10–20 %), no fee — under a purchase's cost, so no order is filled at a profit from the Crier's own goods; its window: the good's class (`window`: raw goods and plates 20–40 minutes, glass 30–60, wire and parts 60–120 — each outlasts a night). One draw in ten (`eventChance`, from level 2) is an **event order**: ×`eventWorth` the size, ×1.5 the window, always a special reward; others carry one with `rewardChance` (20 %). A **reward** is a bonus in ocelles paid with the order (`bonus`, by level; never AI tokens: no reserve of them, owner), a building kit (`ORDER_KITS`: a building's materials ×n into the Core, the latest kits the base has unlocked), or — an event's — a research node the tree offers (`researchBlock` null or held only by a lab, a slot or resources), learnt at once.
- **Accepted** by the player: `acceptDelivery(id)`, the logged `{ k: 'acceptDelivery', id }` — its window starts (`due` = the epoch now + `windowMin`). Refused for an order not standing or already accepted. An offer nobody accepts is withdrawn at `offerEnd` (`offerMin`, 45 minutes) without a word.
- **Filled through a post** (`fillAtPost`, called by each post in `tradeEpoch` before its sales): what its entrance brought (`sellingOf`, D131) of an accepted order's item goes to the order first, whole items within the post's lift budget, the orders in the order they were posted; counted in the post's lift (`soldMu`) and `soldTotal`, never in `t.sold` — off the market, as the starter contract: no price moves. The rest is sold as ever (floor prices do not hold an order back).
- **Closed** in `deliveryEpoch`: filled (`got = n`) — paid `n × cents` in full (`pay`: `credits`, `earned`, the purse's `delivery` kind, a `delivery` ledger line from the Core), its reward delivered —; its window run out — what it got paid at the boundary's bid less the post's fee, as a sale would have; a missed order simply closes, its premium lost, nothing else —; each with a `delivery` event (`posted`, `filled`, `missed`, `withdrawn`). A research learnt as a reward rebuilds the Mods: `tradeEpoch` returns true and the catch-up rebuilds its graph.
- **Offline**: nothing of its own. Every step runs at the boundary in `tradeEpoch` — the same call online and in the catch-up —, draws from the seeds and the exact state (`done`, the Core's level, the quests, the prices; never a stock level), fills from the posts' entrances (`tradeRes`, re-classified after the boundary like a sale); the catch-up drops its events (`dropTradeEvents`). Bit for bit with ticking ([orders.test.ts](../src/sim/orders.test.ts)).
- Tests: [orders.test.ts](../src/sim/orders.test.ts) — the first order after the tutorial, the cadence and the three at most; the draws deterministic, feasible at the Core's level (a starting kit gets only coal and iron ore), bigger at a higher level, never priced over a purchase; filled before the sale within the lift; paid in full at the boundary with its reward (a kit, a bonus, a node; never tokens); missed and paid at the market; an offer withdrawn; the command's replay; the save by id; online ≈ offline over two hours, filled and missed, bit for bit.

### The claims' standings (D153)

« Classement des concessions »: the claim's value (D50, [value.ts](../src/sim/systems/value.ts)) ranked against the forty claims of the Crier. Rules in [systems/ranking.ts](../src/sim/systems/ranking.ts), numbers in `BAL.ranking`.

- **The other claims' values** (`rivals`, `rivalValue`): from the seeds alone, as the market's colonies (`COLONY_PROFILES`, the same order as `makeColonies`). At the player's founding a ladder from `floor` (1 500 ◉) up by `step` (×1.194) a rank — the leader ≈ 1.5 M ◉ —, each colony placed on it by `hash32(worldSeed ^ salt, colony)`; each grows linearly by `g` ‰ of that value a day — from `growth[0]` (40 %) at the foot to `growth[1]` (4 %) at the top, × its profile's growth against `growthRef` — with a day-to-day `wobble` (±6 %) eased hour by hour. Counted from the player's founding, as the market counts its colonies' growth. Integers (`mulDiv`).
- **The standings** (`rankEpoch`, from `Sim.tradeEpoch` after the value's sample): drawn up at a base's first boundary, then each world hour (`absEpoch % everyMin`): the claim's value then against every claim's (`standings`: richest first, the player ahead of a tie), its rank and best kept in `sim.ranking` (saved, a statistic: not hashed, as the value it ranks). A climb is a `rank` event naming the claims passed. Offline the same call at the same boundaries; the value itself may count a few items a line just took from a small store (offline.md), so the rank is offline-consistent, not bit-exact.
- Tests: [ranking.test.ts](../src/sim/ranking.test.ts) — the ladder and its order, the growth and wobble, the standings' order and the player's place (a new base last, a few hours' base mid-table, the leaders out of reach for weeks), the hourly update and the climb's event, the save, two hours caught up alike.

## Tick anatomy

`Sim.step()`, in order:

1. **Constructions**: entities whose `builtAt ≤ tick` become built (`dirty`, `jobsDirty`, a `structure` event).
2. **Recompile** if `dirty` (`ensureCompiled`).
3. **Power**: `ctx.now = tick`; per grid, last tick's demand against this epoch's weather → `ctx.sat`, `ctx.dispatch`, accumulator charge.
4. **Flow phases** A1 → A2 → A3 → B → C over the whole graph (`ctx.glean` set first: at night the Gleaners take from the exposed ribbons in C; their thefts go to the marks after, D103).
5. **Research** (`researchTick`): the servers' output for the tick (`serverTick`) and each lab's power (`labTick`: a project's `lit`; a reconversion's time); finish a reconversion that has run out. At the epoch boundary, after the trade and the leases, `tokenEpoch` delivers the minute's tokens (the Core's compute, the servers', the bank, the bought flows billed), the projects they complete finish, and a queued node whose resources are in starts (D122).
6. **Landscape**: assign jobs (`jobsDirty` or `tick % EPOCH === 0`), advance one tick, complete finished jobs.
7. `tick++`; when the new tick is a multiple of `EPOCH`: **trade** (`systems/trade.ts` `tradeEpoch`: deliveries, orders, CrierBelt's delivery orders filled from each post's entrance then its sales, the delivery orders closed and posted (D153), the auto-sales, the cargo, the market's settlement → the next epoch's prices; then the claim's value and standings when due, D50, D153), then **the leases' hour** (`systems/land.ts` `landEpoch`: each lease due pays its rent or lapses), then the **tokens' minute** (`tokenEpoch`, then `finishProjects` for a project whose tokens came in, D118, then `startQueued` when `queuedReady`, D122), then **the expertises' minute** (`systems/expertise.ts` `expertiseEpoch`, D93: the Core's grid, ranks, a réorientation's end; the Mods rebuilt when one in place changed), then **the Gleaners' nightfall and dawn** (`systems/gleaners.ts` `gleanEpoch`, D103), then push `{ k: 'epoch', epoch: absEpoch }`. The next tick uses the new epoch's weather.
8. **Quests** (`systems/quests.ts` `questStep`): at an epoch boundary the current quest's rate windows close; every `QUEST_CHECK_TICKS` (10) ticks its objectives are checked and, when they hold, it completes: reward into the Hearth, a logged `quest` command, a `quest` event ([quests.md](quests.md)).

## Statistics

`sim.produced` / `sim.consumed` are cumulative milli-item counters (saved, not hashed), and `sim.gleaned` what the Gleaners took (D103): machines count inputs at cycle start and outputs at emission, drills their output, generators their fuel, research its payments, jobs their stone, digs their embedded ore. The UI samples them into rate windows ([stats.ts](../src/ui/stats.ts), see [ui.md](ui.md)). `sim.hearthIn` (D79) counts only what the base brings into the Hearth while ticking — its ports' intake (`ReservoirNode.hearth`, through `TickCtx.hearthIn`), dug tiles' ore, a lost trade post's deliveries — never what commands spend or refund; neither saved nor hashed, the catch-up leaves its ports' share alone (its reservoirs are virtual). The HUD's rates read it; `Chain.pushedTotal` feeds the Logistique view's heat (D88: `readouts/ribbons.ts`); `sim.netPower` holds last tick's demand/supply per grid for the HUD. `totalHeld(item)` sums the Hearth, stores, buffers and ribbons: the conservation test checks `Δheld = Δproduced − Δconsumed` exactly.

### The purse's journal (D102)

`sim.purseLog` ([purse.ts](../src/sim/purse.ts)): every change of `sim.credits`, summed by what made it since the colony was opened — recurring `sales`, `orders` (every trade ledger line books its cents: `line()` in systems/trade.ts), `autoSell` (the Core's surplus, D127: its `autoSell` ledger lines), `tokens`, `fee`, `rental` (`tokenEpoch`), `rent` (a lease's hours, its first one included); one-offs `spotSell`, `spotBuy`, `land` (bought, sold), `reorient`, `reward` (a quest's credits), `grant` (`grantCredits`), `delivery` (a CrierBelt order paid, D153). Each place that changes the purse calls `book(sim, kind, cents)` beside it, so the kinds add up to the purse's change to the cent, online and through the catch-up, which runs the same trade, token and lease functions ([purse.test.ts](../src/sim/purse.test.ts)). Neither saved nor hashed, like `hearthIn`: the HUD samples it once a game minute for its real ◉/h ([ui.md](ui.md#the-purses-card-d102)). **A new way to change the purse books its kind** (a new kind in `PURSE_KINDS`, recurring or not in `RECURRING`).

## The state hash

`Sim.hash()` feeds a `StateHasher` (two 32-bit lanes, [hash.ts](../src/core/hash.ts)) with: tick, credits, Hearth level and stock; every entity (id, def, x, y, rot, cfg, builtAt) in id order; stores, the trade posts' entrances (`selling`, D131) and accumulator energy by id; the power wires (their count, then each pair, low id first, ascending); the unlock tree's completed nodes, plans and research (`done`, sorted), notes, respec state; projects (id, lab, dur, el, paid, tokens, tokIn, buy, lit); the tokens' state (`serverAcc`, `researchHeld`, `tokenPlan`, `tokenBank`); marks; jobs; the land as changed by play (terrain, level and ore diffs; the parcels bought and granted, sorted; each lease with its due tick and rent); the trade, CrierBelt's delivery orders standing (D153; not their history) and the market's own state; the tutorial's quest state (done, baselines, rate windows, `named`, waived fees, titles); the expertises (ranks, slots, réorientations, the grid's run and best); then every node's `hash(h, now)` in graph order. Node hashes use ticks **relative to `now`** (chains hash due runs as one canonical run) so equal dynamics give equal hashes at different times — the catch-up's period detection relies on it. Reservoir nodes hash nothing online (their levels are in `stock`/`stores`). Not hashed: `ents.nextId`, project costs and notes, the name, statistics, the log. `StateHasher.int` splits a number into two 32-bit words and drops any fraction. Invariant: same founding + same commands (+ same catch-ups) ⇒ same hash.

## Serialization

[serialize.ts](../src/sim/serialize.ts) produces plain JSON-able `SaveData`; the persist layer gzips and stores it ([persistence.md](persistence.md)). It carries its content table (`content`), and `deserialize` re-points it to today's content first ([save-remap.ts](../src/sim/save-remap.ts), D47); a saved ribbon line that no longer compiles the same hands its items to the Heart.

| Saved | Rebuilt on load |
|---|---|
| Seeds, `foundingEpoch`, name, tick, credits, Hearth level and id, `nextId` | Generated terrain (from the `TerrainSource`), occupancy |
| Entities packed 7 ints each, `portItems` | The starting square and the neighbouring bases (their parcels and buildings, from the land and the seeds, D110), `Mods` |
| Hearth stock, `stores`, the trade posts' entrances (`selling`, D131), accumulator energy, `done` (plans and research), the power wires (`links`: flat pairs, optional) | The graph, `ctx`, grids (the wires' components) |
| `terrainDiff`, `levelDiff`, `oreDiff`, `land` (bought, granted, leases: D60) | `jobsDirty = true`, `dirty = true` |
| Non-reservoir node snapshots by entity id; chains by `tier:head:len`: a ribbon's `cells` and `stage`, a conduit's `runs` (absolute ticks) | |
| `produced`, `consumed`; projects, notes, respecs, marks, jobs, the quests (by id), the expertises (D93), the Gleaners' counts and marks (D103: `gleaners`, statistics), the blueprints' library (D154: `blueprints`, by content ids, not hashed) (optional fields) | The command log, events, the Gleaners' reach (`Graph.glean`) |

Guards: `load(save(sim)).hash() === sim.hash()` and a loaded colony continues exactly like the original ([serialize.test.ts](../src/sim/serialize.test.ts)); the same mid-dig ([space.test.ts](../src/sim/space.test.ts)).

**Adding a saved field without breaking old saves**: add it to `SaveData` as an **optional** field, write it in `serialize`, read it with a default (`d.x ?? default`) in `deserialize` — the pattern used by `projects`, `designated`, `jobs`. Add it to `Sim.hash()` if it changes the dynamics, and extend the two round-trip tests. Declare it in `FIELDS` (save-remap.ts). A change that cannot default (renamed or re-shaped data, new node snapshot shapes) needs `SAVE_VERSION` bumped and a step in `MIGRATIONS` ([persist/migrate.ts](../src/persist/migrate.ts)); `deserialize` itself throws on any other version.

## Determinism rules

- In `src/sim`, `src/content` and the four core helpers: no `Math.random`, no `Date.now` / `performance.now` / `new Date(`, no transcendental `Math` (`sin`, `cos`, `exp`, `log`, `pow`, `sqrt`, `hypot`, `atan2`…), no `**` with a non-literal exponent. Use `isqrt`, `mulDiv`, d², integer tables. The market (`src/market`, M6) may compute in floats but must not read randomness or the clock either. Both rules are the **determinism guard** tests in [serialize.test.ts](../src/sim/serialize.test.ts), which scan the sources (details in [testing.md](testing.md)).
- Iterate in canonical orders: entities by id, tiles by index, Maps/Sets in a deterministic insertion order, sorts with total comparators; never `for…in`. Fairness uses `now % n`, never a hidden counter.
- State is integers; floats only as deterministic IEEE intermediates floored at once. Randomness is stateless: `hash32(seed, …)`.
- The shell owns time and calls `step` / `catchUp`; the sim never reads a clock.

**Purity guard**: [src/sim/tsconfig.json](../src/sim/tsconfig.json) compiles the engine, `src/market` (M6) and `src/content` with `lib: ES2022` and no ambient types (tests, benches and workers excluded); `npm run typecheck` runs it. Any DOM or Node global (`document`, `performance`, `process`…), or an import of a DOM-using module such as `core/dom.ts`, fails the typecheck. Which layers the sim may import at all (core, content, market) is enforced separately by [architecture.test.ts](../src/architecture.test.ts) ([../ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md)); by convention the sim never imports the event bus. This keeps the engine runnable in a Worker (the catch-up, M7) and on a Node server.

## Adding a new building kind (engine side)

1. **Content**: the definition in [buildings.ts](../src/content/buildings.ts) (kind, footprint, ports for rot 0, `kw`/`idleKw`, cost, unlock, `needsZone`: whether it stands on the claim's land); a new `BuildingKind` in [content/types.ts](../src/content/types.ts). See [content.md](content.md).
2. **Placement**: special terrain or needs go in `build.canPlace` ([systems/build.ts](../src/sim/systems/build.ts)) with a `PlaceReason`. If the building may be placed over others, or have others placed over it, give it a `replaceGroup` (see *Placement and replacement*). If turning it changes nothing (no ports, square, not a line, not directional), `turns` already treats a re-placement as identical.
3. **Node**: a `case` in `compileGraph`'s switch. Prefer an existing class (`ReservoirNode` for anything that stores, `MachineNode`, `DrillNode`, `ConsumerNode`). Create through `reuse(e, make, same)` and set mutable parameters (kw, speed, rate) *after* `reuse`, since a reused node keeps its old ones.
4. **New node class** ([nodes.ts](../src/sim/flow/nodes.ts)): add its `NodeKind`; implement the phase methods reading only start-of-tick state; set `status`; make `demand()` a function of hashed state; `hash(h, now)` with relative ticks only; `snapshot()`/`restore(s, shift)` shifting absolute ticks; any `now % n` rotation must be reflected in `Group.rotL` ([groups.ts](../src/sim/graph/groups.ts)) and its parameters in `signature`. Add its buffers to `refundNode` (compile.ts) and to `heldIn` ([state.ts](../src/sim/state.ts)).
5. **Power**: a building with `kw`, `idleKw`, `gen` or `accumulator` takes part in power (`linkable`) and runs only once the player wires it to a grid; a new link point is a `pole: { range, slots }` (with `tall` for the xp4 bonus). Producers are listed in compile's grid section (a new producer kind needs a `Producer.kind` and a `producerKw` case in [network.ts](../src/sim/power/network.ts), reading the `Weather`); fuel burners are `GeneratorNode`s.
6. **Removal and refunds**: `build.removeEntity` refunds what it cost (`refundCost`: its price when it went up, D123), node buffers, stores, modules (and `Sim.remove` cuts its wires); add anything new it owns.
7. **Save/hash**: non-reservoir node snapshots are saved automatically by entity id; per-entity data outside nodes needs a saved field and a hash entry.
8. **Offline**: a reservoir is a group boundary; a node inside a group is simulated by warm-ups for free if it follows the rules above. Long processes: next section.
9. **Tests**: conservation (`Δheld = produced − consumed`), order independence, save → load → continue, an online ≈ offline twin ([testing.md](testing.md)). Rendering and UI: [render.md](render.md), [ui.md](ui.md).

## Adding a new long-running process

For anything that runs over minutes or hours (a new kind of job, a converter with a calendar duration, market deliveries…):

1. **State**: a record in the `Sim` (like `Project`, `Job`), saved as an optional `SaveData` field and hashed.
2. **Rule**: a pure function over integers in the allowance style — `el += sat × ticks`; due = `mulDiv(total, el, dur·1000) − moved`; move `min(due, available)`. It must give the same result for one call over N ticks as for N calls over one tick whenever the store does not limit it.
3. **Store**: attach it to the building's `ReservoirNode` store, never to a node inside a group.
4. **Online**: a system function called at a fixed place of `step` (after the phases), one tick at a time; commands to start/stop it in [commands.ts](../src/sim/commands.ts) (`Command`, `exec`, logged); events in `SimEvent`.
5. **Offline**: declare its rate as a pseudo-flow, its completion as an event, advance it per segment with the same function, handle its completion — the checklist at the end of [offline.md](offline.md).
6. **Tests**: a twin test (`a.run(N)` vs `catchUp(b, N, { noDirect: true })` from one save).

## Known gaps / pitfalls

- **Sharing is greedy round-robin, not water-filling**: `waterfill` in `core/fixed.ts` is tested but unused. Forks split evenly only over time.
- **Wires recompile the graph**: `link` / `unlink` set `dirty`, so a wire costs a full recompile like any structural change (tens of ms on a 25 k base). Saves from before D25 carry no wires: such a colony stands unpowered until wired (no migration; persistence is M7).
- **No incremental compile**: every recompile rebuilds the whole graph (and, offline, re-warms every group); there is no "incremental ≡ full" test. (A chain reuses an old chain's state only when its whole tile path is identical; a fingerprint match alone used to duplicate items — guarded by "a re-laid chain … keeps exactly its items" in `flow.test.ts`.)
- **Hash blind spots**: project costs are not hashed, and `StateHasher.int` drops fractions, so a fractional milli-item would go unseen.
- **Trade simplifications** (M6): trading does not depend on the post's power (it only draws kW); spot trades do not consume the post's throughput; the plan's forward contracts (cm4), colony charters (cm7), the Crier House's limit orders (cmK) and the 6 h forecast (cm1) are not built.
- **Not wired yet**: `energyKwTicks` (the cell charger's 600 kW·min per craft); `MachineNode` offers only `outs[0]` and its waste bin (content has single-output recipes, unchecked; the waste is `RecipeDef.waste`, D125); mods `tradeFee`, `deliveryDelay`, `slippage`, `streams` (M6); flags `tunnels`, `stormHarvest`, `deepHeat` and the market flags; `oreDiff`/`setOre`.
- Fixed in the architecture pass (kept here as a record): the land's embedded-copper chance now comes from its archetype (`TerrainSource.embedCopper`); finished remote constructions push a `structure` event and wake idle excavators; `place` keeps only a valid recipe / filters from its `cfg` (`validCfg`); `NodeKind` lists only the kinds node classes use; `foundingEpoch` counts minutes since the Unix epoch (≥ `WORLD_EPOCH0`).
- Refunding a crafting machine (`refundNode`) returns inputs already counted in `consumed`: the conservation identity only holds between recompiles.
- **Replacement**: a refund is the base cost, so turning or upgrading a ribbon off the claim's land loses its ×3 remote surcharge each time. A replacement off the claim's land is a new construction site, and the line stays cut until its kite arrives. There is no liquid junction: a conduit dragged across a conduit line turns that conduit, because the junction is `transport` and cannot replace a conduit. A drag's shared budget counts only the construction costs refunded, not the items in a replaced block's buffer, so a drag may stop one tile earlier than the stock would strictly allow (`placeBelts` follows the plan).
- The command log is not saved; a loaded colony cannot be replayed from founding. Catch-ups are not logged either ([offline.md](offline.md)).
- `npm run bench` runs the perf test (`vitest run src/sim/bench`).
