# DOM UI (`src/ui`)

The interface over the world is plain DOM: inked paper **cartouches** (HUD, build bar, inspector, toasts, the build tip) and full **screens** (the main menu with its settings and credits pages, research, stats, the Crier, the dev art board). Components read the colony every 0.25 s and write the DOM only when something changed. They act only through the Sim's command methods or the host closures the shell gives them. The engine never knows the UI exists.

## Page structure

`index.html`, back to front: `#stage` (the Pixi canvas) · `#ui` (HUD, inspector, the land panel, the views' card, build bar, toasts) · `#screens` (one full screen at a time) · the tutorial's guide (z 30, over the screens), the building card (z 32: the player is reading it), the naming modal (z 35), only while a reveal plays the spotlight (z 38), and while it is open the item picker's list (z 45, [Item mentions and the item picker](#item-mentions-and-the-item-picker)), appended to `body` · `#tooltip` (the build tip) · `#err` (fatal errors, also caught by the smoke test). Every component root carries `data-screen="…"`, which the smoke test and the CSS use. `--ui-scale` (from the settings) zooms `#ui` and `#screens` (CSS `zoom` in `menu.css`, which also pins their base font-size so text is not scaled twice); `#tooltip` stays at 1 because it is placed in screen pixels from the pointer. `body[data-menu]` is set while the main menu is open: `#ui` fades out.

## Patterns

**Build once, update cheaply.** A component builds its DOM with `el(tag.class, attrs, children)` (`on*` attributes become listeners). Then `refresh()` (called at 4 Hz by the shell) writes values with `setText` / `setAttr`. These remember the last value per node and skip unchanged writes. Never rebuild a subtree on every refresh: it costs layout, and it throws away focus and open `<select>`s.

**Progress bars** (owner, 2026-10-13: "put a little progress bar to show the progression of the current task"): each machine's hero shows its current craft (« Fabrication », under its recipe's equation), each drill or sand collector the next item it cuts (« Extraction »), and each research lab its project. `progressBar()` in [inspector/kit.ts](../src/ui/inspector/kit.ts) scales its fill (a transform) and tints it by the building's state (`STATUS_TONE`). It is full while the output is full and empty while the building waits for its inputs. A bar moves **every frame**: panels register per-frame updaters through `PanelCtx.anim`, which `Inspector.frame(simTicks)` runs from `App.frame` while the inspector is open. `smoothProgress` interpolates between the sim's 10 Hz ticks from the last tick's step, across the wrap from one craft to the next ([progress.test.ts](../src/ui/inspector/progress.test.ts)).

**Signatures decide rebuilds.** When the structure of a panel depends on state (the inspector's panels, the research cards, the build tray), compute a short signature string of what the structure depends on. Rebuild only when it changes; anything else goes through live updaters. Example: `Inspector.signature()` covers entity id, `cfg`, construction, graph node, locale, port items, Hearth level, fleet size and research count.

**Hosts, not imports.** A component receives a small host interface (`HudHost`, `BuildBarHost`, `InspectorHost`, `LandHost`, `ViewBarHost`, `ViewPanelHost`, `ResearchHost`, `StatsHost`) with exactly the actions it may trigger: open a screen, select, remove, notify. It never imports the shell or the scene graph (`src/architecture.test.ts`: ui may use render's art kit and geometry only, never `pixi.js`).

**Commands for changes.** Buttons call the Sim's command methods (`sim.upgradeHearth()`, `sim.setRecipe(...)`, `sim.addRoute(...)`…) or host actions. A refusal comes back as a result (`{ ok, reason }` or `false`); show it with a toast, using the `reason.*` i18n keys.

**Icons come from the atlas.** `itemIcon(atlas, item, px)` and `buildingIcon(atlas, def, px)` copy atlas frames into small canvases. The art is the same as in the world, pixel for pixel ([render.md](render.md)). **An item named in text always shows its icon**: build it with `itemMention(item)`, or pass `itemRef(item)` as the placeholder of a translated sentence ([Item mentions and the item picker](#item-mentions-and-the-item-picker)); never write an item's bare name where the player reads it.

**Text through i18n.** Every string uses `t()` / `tr()` (plurals) with keys from `src/i18n/fr.ts`. Content names come from the tables (`itemDef(i).name[locale()]`). Numbers go through `Intl` (`fmt` in the inspector kit, `format.ts` helpers). Mind the French typography rules ([i18n.md](i18n.md)).

## The build bar

[build-bar.ts](../src/ui/build-bar.ts) shows **only what the colony has unlocked** (the owner's rule, [D27](decisions.md)): no greyed-out or transparent entries.

- **Trays.** `trayOf(cat)` is every building of a category except the Hearth, in content order; `unlockedTray(sim, cat)` keeps those whose `unlock` the colony has (`sim.unlocked`). A tray shows `unlockedTray` only.
- **Tabs.** `shownCategories(sim)`: the categories with at least one unlocked building. The others get the `hidden` attribute (no tab at all).
- **Hotkeys follow what is shown.** Shift+n opens the n-th tab shown (`BuildBar.categoryAt`, used by `BuildMode.slot`), and the tab's key cap says `⇧n`; 1…9, 0 take the visible slots in order (`slotDef`). At the start only the drill, the ribbon and the pole are unlocked (D33), so a new base shows three tabs (Extraction, Logistique, Énergie); the others appear as plans unlock their buildings (Distribution with the router, Production with the smelter…). During the tutorial the bar as a whole waits for quest 1 (the `build` reveal, D32); past that its tabs follow the unlocks only.
- **Something unlocks.** The bar compares the unlocked set at each 4 Hz refresh (`syncUnlocks`; the first look is the baseline, and a swapped colony starts a new one). A newly unlocked building raises a notice « Nouveau : X » (`BuildBarHost.notify`, several names joined by `Intl.ListFormat`), gets a « Nouveau » tag on its slot (`data-new`) and a coral dot on its tab while the tab is closed. The tag goes once the slot is hovered or picked, or when its tray closes (it has been seen).
- **Language.** Tab and button labels are re-set on each refresh, and the tray is rebuilt when the language changes (its signature holds the category, the locale and the unlocked set), so a switch from the settings shows at once.
- **Icons.** `buildingIcon` copies the building's plan frame (`btKey(def, 0)`). Ribbons and conduits have no plan frame (they are baked as belt pieces): their straight piece (`beltKey(tier, false)`), upright, one tile wide at the scale of a 1×1 frame, repeated down the icon.
- **Slots** carry `data-def` (the building's hover card finds them by event delegation) and no native `title`, which would double that card.
- **The Link tool slot.** The Énergie tray ends with « Relier » (`data-tool="link"`, key cap L, an inked two-pillars-and-a-wire icon drawn in code): `BuildBarHost.link()` takes / puts away the power-wire tool (D25), `linking()` lights the slot (`data-on`). It is not a building: no cost, no digit.
- **Layout.** The bar spans the window (`left` / `right` 12 px) and centres its rows: one row of tabs, the tray on one row of up to ten. The empty sides let clicks through to the world.
- **Only while building** (D88; owner, 2026-10-15: « plus séparé »). With no category open the bar shows nothing (`.buildbar:not([data-open]) .bb-tabs`, CSS only: the tabs keep their `hidden` attribute for what is unlocked, which the smoke counts). « Construire », B or a digit opens the last category; Shift+n the n-th. What a tray holds new shows as a coral dot on « Construire » (`hasNew` → `HudHost.buildNew`), and the tutorial's guide points at « Construire » before a tab (guide.ts `build`).

Other places checked for locked constructions: the tech tree shows what a node unlocks (that is its job), the dev art board shows everything, the inspector's recipe buttons and the Crier's « Ce qui rapporte » list only unlocked recipes, the build tip only shows what is in hand, and `BuildMode.pick` still refuses a locked building with a toast (the pipette, `FL.tool`).

## The tech tree screen

[screens/research.ts](../src/ui/screens/research.ts) (ScreenId `research`; T, or the HUD's tree chip) draws the one unlock tree of D33 ([content.md](content.md#the-unlock-tree--plans-and-research-d33)). It is a wide canvas in a scrollable view: drag the background to pan, the wheel scrolls it sideways when there is nothing to scroll up and down; a detail pane sits on the right. Styles: the plans and the shared rules in `screens.css`, research's half and the gate in [research.css](../src/ui/styles/research.css). Show, don't tell (D53): icons, bars and badges carry the costs; the words are in the tooltips and the detail pane.

- **Layout** (`treeLayout`, computed once from content), left to right: the **plans** block (warm), the **gate**, the **research** block (cool).
  - *Plans*: a node's column is its longest path from the root, « Le Cœur » with its starting kit (the Heart's icon, the drill, the ribbon); lanes are the six **paths** of one theme each (D41, `PLAN_PATHS`: Production, Logistique, Énergie, Analytique, Commerce, Arpentage). Within a lane a node takes the free row nearest its parents', so paths run straight. Rows are compact (56 px nodes, 66 px rows) so the six paths fit a 900 px window.
  - *The gate* (D71): the research lab's plan (`lane: 'gate'`, not a path) stands in its own column between the blocks, level with the Heart. It needs every other plan: each path's last node(s) — its plans in its last column — link into it, along their own row to the block's edge and then converging (`Link.path`: dashed once that whole path is taken). A caption over its card says « Tous les plans » with a « n/N » chip (`.tr-gatecap`, ✓ when all are taken). Its card (`[data-lane="gate"]`, a lilac ring) shows the lab and the data collector it unlocks, its cost and the « Débloquer » tab; its tooltip says « Il faut d’abord tous les plans : n/N plans pris ».
  - *Research* (D69) reads in four **tier columns** (`.tr-tiercol`, Tier 1 … Tier 4), each a few sub-columns wide (a node's sub-column is its chain of same-lane, same-tier prerequisites). Each column's head (`.tr-tierhead[data-tier]`) says « Tier n », « Ouvert par » and a pill naming what opens it — the lab's plan for Tier 1, then `tr1`, `tr6`, `tr8` (`TIER_GATE`; they stand in the column before the tier they open), padlocked until taken, ✓ after, a click selects it. The trunk, « Fondations », runs on top (its longest line on the first row); under it one lane per family (`FAMILY_IDS`), whose name changes with the tier column (`familyName`: a tag on the band's top edge over each column, `.tr-ttag`) and which splits in two **specialities** from Tier 3 (`FAMILIES[f].specs`): two sub-lanes with a dashed divider (`.tr-split`) carrying the second one's name, the family's shared nodes centred between them so their links fork.
- **Paths and lanes**: every lane has a colour (`--path` on `[data-lane]`: production rust, logistics blue, energy gold, analytics teal, trade coral, survey olive; the trunk plum, the families their own, the gate lilac). A lane is a **band** (`.tr-band`) washed in its colour and a **label** as tall as the band (`.tr-lane[data-lane]`): a plan path's name, what it is for (`tree.path.<lane>`) and a « n/of » chip (`.tr-pathcount`, ✓ once whole); a research lane's name (« Fondations », or the family's), its specialities (or its last tier's name), « n/of » learnt and, for a family, « Reconvertir » (armed by a first click).
- **Links**: SVG curves from a prerequisite's right edge to what it opens — inked once done, dashed turquoise while the way is open, faint dotted while locked. ## The spotlight (D41)

[focus.ts](../src/ui/focus.ts) is a short « black out » that shows the player what just arrived and where it lives (owner, 2026-10-12: "a little 'black out' of the game's content so that the user can focus on the new analytics he has. Little animation to show the change"). It is generic: a `FocusShot` is an element (or a selector, resolved when it plays), a kicker, CrierBelt's line and a skip hint; the HUD plays one for each analytics plan (`ANALYTICS_REVEALS` in [analytics.ts](../src/ui/analytics.ts)), and any other unlock can queue its own.

- **What it does** (`focusTiming`; it holds 2 s, then waits for the player — owner, 2026-10-13): the whole game dims (the world, the HUD, the bars: the dim is the hole's own shadow, spread over the window, so it covers every layer whatever its stacking) while an iris closes from the window onto the element (0.42 s); an inked ring with a peach glow stamps around it and pulses twice; the caption cartouche (`.focus-cap`: « Analytique · Registre de production », a coral « CrierBelt » tag and the line, « Clic ou Échap pour continuer ») slides in under the element, or over it near the bottom of the window (`captionAt`). After the 2 s hold the root gets `data-ready` and the hint shows; the next click or key fades the layer out and removes it. The element is re-measured every frame, so a HUD that re-lays itself out keeps its hole. A cue plays (`notice`).
- **When**: `Hud.refresh` asks `UnlockWatch` which analytics plans were just taken and queues their shots; `Spotlight.pump(clear)` plays the next one only while nothing covers the HUD (`HudHost.covered`: a full screen — the tree the plan was bought in, the menu, the intro — or the naming modal). So a plan bought in the tree plays when the player closes it, on the base, with the new chip already there. Several queue and play one after the other. A colony seen for the first time (a loaded or swapped base) is a baseline: nothing replays.
- **Read, not missed**: during the first 2 s nothing ends it; after, a click anywhere or any key does (`dismiss`). A click, Esc, Enter and Space are always swallowed (Esc does not also open the menu or close a tool); any other key reaches the game, before and after. `skip()` ends it at once for the shell and `FL.focusReveal(null)`.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion`): no iris, stamp, pulse or slide — the dim and the words fade in and out (0.15 s / 0.2 s) with the same time to read.
- **Automation**: under `navigator.webdriver` (the smoke test, the shot scripts) a queued shot is dropped: reveals never play by themselves. `FL.focusReveal(id)` plays an analytics plan's reveal anyway (`play(shot, true)`), `FL.focusReveal(null)` skips it, `FL.focusReveal()` reads the state. Hooks: `[data-screen="focus"][data-focus="<id>"][data-phase="in|hold|out"]`, `.focus-hole`, `.focus-ring`, `.focus-cap`.
- **Another unlock**: build a `FocusShot` (a stable `data-*` hook on the element to lift, its words through `t()`), call `spotlight.queue(shot)` when it unlocks (with your own `UnlockWatch` if it is a plan), and let the 4 Hz `pump` play it.

## The building card

[building-card.ts](../src/ui/building-card.ts) answers « what does this do? » before the player builds. When the pointer rests on a tray slot (`.bb-slot[data-def]`, about a quarter of a second) or a slot takes the keyboard focus, an inked card opens above the tray, centred on the slot, its tail pointing at it. Once a card is open, the next slot's opens at once, so sweeping along the tray reads like flipping cards; it closes a moment after the pointer leaves the slots, or when the tray closes or changes. It listens on the bar's root by event delegation (`new BuildingCard(atlas, host, bar.root)` in the shell), so the bar knows nothing of it, and it never takes the pointer. The shell calls its `refresh()` at 4 Hz with the other cartouches: the facts are recomputed and compared whole (a research that lands or the language rebuild it), and the cost chips turn coral when the Heart runs short. It closes when a full screen or the menu comes over the bar (`BuildingCardHost.covered`). It lives on `body` at z-index 32, above the tutorial's guide and below the naming modal and the tip, and zooms itself with `--ui-scale` as `#ui` does; it converts screen pixels into its zoomed ones when it places itself.

The description is shown nowhere else in full (D53): the **inspector** shows its first sentence only for a kind with no hero, no panel and no grid card, and the **build tip** none of it.

### Writing a good `desc`

- **One or two plain sentences.** The first says what the building does and must stand on its own: the build tip shows only it. The second says how to hook it up, or the one catch: which side takes what (« Amène minerai et charbon à ses deux entrées, à l’arrière »), where it must stand (« Se pose uniquement sur un évent »), what to set in the inspector, what it may be placed over.
- **Only the numbers that decide** (20/min, 300 kW, rayon 12). The card already shows the figures from the tables; a number you quote is not checked, so keep it in step with the content and `BAL`.
- **The game's words, to the player** (« tu »): ruban, Cœur, parcelle, Aménager, Relier, données (the land's own texts — sold by CrierBelt — say « vous », « vos terres », as the research does). The lore applies ([lore.md](lore.md)): no people at the base, « ta base ».
- **French typography**: U+00A0 before `:`, U+202F before `; ! ? %` and as the thousands separator (« 1 000 »), U+00A0 between a number and its unit (« 300 kW »). `i18n.test.ts` checks the punctuation of every French `desc`; `building-facts.test.ts` checks that each is one or two sentences.

## The inspector and its panels

[inspector/inspector.ts](../src/ui/inspector/inspector.ts) lays every building out the same way, with the [panels' kit](#the-panels-kit-d89) (D89; owner, 2026-10-15: « Plus clair, plus séparé »), top to bottom:

1. **The header**: icon, name, its category and size in tiles, ✕. No description: the panels show what it does (D53); only a kind with no hero, no panel and no grid card gets its description's first sentence (`.insp-desc`).
2. **The top block** (`.insp-top`) answers three questions at a glance:
   - *does it work?* The status line (`statusLine`, `.kstatus[data-state]`): a mark and one word coloured by the state — « En marche », « En pause », « À l’arrêt » (`workOf`, [systems/work.ts](../src/sim/systems/work.ts), D83: what the world draws switched off, so the panel and the board agree), « En chantier », « En sommeil » (D80). A kind with no look of work (ribbons, routing blocks, pillars, stores, the Heart, the trade post) takes the word from its engine status and hides the line while it works;
   - *why not?* The reason, as icons after the word: the inputs it waits for, dashed peach (`.kreason[data-k="missing"]`); its full output, ringed coral with a « full » mark; a struck bolt (no power), a cut plug (no wire), an empty slot (no recipe, nothing left to mine), the parcel or the tree for a sleeper;
   - *what does it do?* The kind's **hero** (`HERO` in panels.ts, `.insp-hero[data-hero]`), on a warm wash: a machine's recipe as an icon equation a minute at full speed with its craft's bar; a drill's or pump's ore a minute and the next item's bar; a generator's fuel a minute → kW; a wind turbine's, solar panel's or geothermal plant's kW now « actuelle · nominale 120 kW »; a lab's project, its % and bar (lilac; coral while research is held); a server's tokens a minute; a silo's item and its fill as a meter; a trade post's three facts; the Core's level as steps and its data outlet's equation; a landscaper's job and time left.
3. **The kind's sections** (`PANELS`): its recipe choice, buffers, output, fuel, data, stock, next level, filters, outlets, routes, gates, cargo, flights, orders — each a kit section (`.ksec[data-sec]`), then the **grid card**.
4. **The foot** (`.insp-foot.kfoot`): every action of the panels (`PanelCtx.action`: « Choisir une recherche », « Ouvrir la Criée », « Relier », « Agrandir au niveau n ») and « Démolir » last, on the right (never for the Heart).

**Why, in words** (`.insp-why[data-state]`, under the status line, only while it is not working): the status in words, then what to do or the items by name — « Attend ses entrées · ⟨Charbon⟩ », « Sans fil · Outil Relier (L) : tends-lui un fil. », a sleeping building how to wake it (D80, `Sim.dormancy`: its parcels to buy or lease back, Terrain (I); its research to take again in the tree) (`insp.status.*`, `insp.why.*`). It is tinted by the state (peach, coral, lilac).

**Facets** (D88). A specialised view (Électricité, Logistique, Production; the views session's `src/ui/views`) names its facet through `InspectorHost.facet()` (`'power' | 'logistics' | 'production' | null`). The facet is part of the signature; the root carries `data-facet`. The sections tagged with it (`data-group`: `power` the grid card; `logistics` a ribbon's contents, a store, outlets, a skydock's routes, filters, the trade post's gates; `production` the production kinds' sections) come first, under the top block, and the others fade (`data-dim`: 0.55, full on hover).

**Focus.** A rebuild (a filter set, an outlet chosen: the signature changes) gives the focus back to the control that had it, found by its `data-pick` hook, so a keyboard player can set three filters in a row.

**The grid card** ([inspector/power.ts](../src/ui/inspector/power.ts) `gridCard`, D25, D76; a kit section since D89, `data-group="power"`), only for a building that takes part in power (it makes, uses or stores power, or carries wires), and the same card for all of them, after the kind's panels. Under a producer's formula, the formula as a bar (`[data-pw="yield"]`): a rail as long as the larger of nominal and now, a tick at the nominal, filled to what it gives now in the coefficient's tone; the « Relier » button sits in the foot. Its head: a bolt (`data-power='on'`), a struck bolt and 0 % when its grid has nothing to draw from (`dark`), a cut plug and « Non relié » when no wire joins it to a grid (`unwired`); a struck bolt and « En sommeil » for a sleeping building, cut from every grid whatever its wires (`asleep`, D80: the line under the bar says so instead of asking for a wire); « Réseau »; the grid's satisfaction %. Under it a bar of that and « ↑ 7 178 kW · ↓ 4 549 kW » (supply, demand; on hover in words, and the pillars it hangs on by name, « Relié à : Poteau ×2 »). Then its own part, by role: *a producer* — **« nominale × coef. = actuelle »**, three figures with captions under them; the coefficient a chip toned by its value (turquoise ≥ ×1, peach below, coral at ×0; a fuelled coal generator stays turquoise: it burns only what its grid asks) with an ⓘ: on hover its reasons, on a click they unfold under the formula (`.pw-why`): a turbine's place (« Hauteur : niveau 3 → ×1,8 », the cirque, the plain, the mesa) and the gusts, with the wind's ladder (a bar per place, its own inked); a solar panel's sun at this hour with the sun's curve over the day (midnight to midnight, now inked); the Heart's panel and its isotope core; a coal generator's share of its grid's demand, or its missing fuel; geothermal ×1. An unwired producer shows its rate and 0 kW now. *A consumer* — « Consomme 25 kW » (`node.demand()`). *An accumulator* — its charge bar and %. *A pillar or the Heart* — its wires against its slots (« 3 sur 8 utilisés ») and the « Relier » button (`InspectorHost.link(id)`: the Link tool from it; lit while it is the tool's source, `linkSource()`). An unwired building whose status is `Unpowered` reads « Sans fil : aucun réseau ne l’alimente » instead of « Sans énergie »: the fix is a wire, not more power.

## Item mentions and the item picker

**Mentions** ([mention.ts](../src/ui/mention.ts), owner 2026-10-13: "when in the UI you refer to an item, like iron ore, try to put the icon of the item next to the text"). An item named anywhere in the DOM UI shows its icon beside its name: `span.imention[data-item]`, an `img` of the item's glyph (baked once per item from the atlas into a PNG, so every mention shares the decoded image) and the name, sized in `em` (1.35 em, sitting on the line like a capital) so it follows the text it is in and the UI scale. Three ways in:

| You have | Write | The page shows |
|---|---|---|
| a node to build | `itemMention(i)`; `{ name: false }` for the icon alone (the name as tooltip and `aria-label`); `{ big: true }` | icon + name |
| a translated sentence | `t('toast.delivered', { n, item: itemRef(i) })`, `itemList([...])` for several | the sentence, the item as icon + name |
| text that cannot hold an icon | `plain(text)` | the bare name |

`itemRef` puts a token (`⟪i:iron_ore⟫`) in the text; the **mention layer** (`installMentions(document.body, atlas)`, called by `main.ts` right after the atlas is baked) is a MutationObserver that swaps every token reaching the page for the mention, whoever wrote it; in `title` / `aria-label` / `alt` / `placeholder` attributes and in native form controls it writes the bare name instead. A language switch renames the mentions on the page. Details and rules: [i18n.md](i18n.md#naming-an-item-its-icon-beside-its-name).

Where items are named: the inspector (recipes and their inputs / outputs, buffers, a drill's ore, a generator's fuel, stores, ribbons, filters and outlets, a lab's data, a trade post's gates, streams, orders, flights and store), the quest log (objectives, their own labels and the guide's hints through `markItems`, rewards, info lines), toasts (deliveries, veins, the Gazette, quests, the Crier's trades), the Crier (prices, streams, orders, flights, the board, the Gazette), the statistics, the tech tree (the detail pane's cost: one line per item with what the Heart holds), the building card (its cost, its fuel), the HUD's stock chips (icon, name in the tooltip).

**The item picker** ([item-picker.ts](../src/ui/item-picker.ts)). A native `<option>` cannot hold an image, so an item choice is `itemPicker({ items, value, none, onPick, attrs?, label?, known? })` → `{ root, value, set(i) }`: a `button.ipick` that shows the chosen item (icon + name, or the « none » choice in italics) and opens an inked list (`[data-screen="item-picker"]`, on `<body>` at z-index 45, over the panels and the full screens, zoomed with the UI scale like the building card). With `known` (the kit passes `seenBy(sim)`), the items the base has made, used or held come first and the others follow under « Pas encore vus à ta base ».

- Keyboard: on the button ↓ ↑ Enter Space open the list; in it ↓ ↑ Home End PageUp PageDown move, typing the first letters jumps (accents and case ignored; one letter again cycles), Enter / Space pick, Esc closes (the focus stays on the button), Tab closes and moves on. The keys it uses are stopped there, so they never pan the camera or fire a hotkey, as a focused `<select>` did. ARIA: the button has `aria-haspopup="listbox"`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-activedescendant`; the list is a `listbox` of `option`s.
- Mouse: a click opens or closes; hovering moves the highlight; a click picks; a press elsewhere closes.
- For the guide and scripts the button keeps a `<select>`-like surface: `value` is the item id (`''` for none), `data-item` too, and the hooks passed in `attrs` sit on it (`data-outlet`, `data-fork-side`, `data-export-slot`, `data-order-item`, `data-spot-item`, `data-pick` for the inspector's focus).
- `set(i)` shows another item without calling `onPick` (the colony changed it: a refused choice goes back). The list closes by itself when its button leaves the page (an inspector rebuilt).

## Recipes

**Add an inspector panel for a kind**
1. Write the panel in the group file that fits (`production.ts`, `logistics.ts`, `colony.ts`), or in a new one (M6: `trade.ts` for the trade post): kit sections (`section`, with its `group` when it belongs to a view's facet), meters, equations; its buttons through `ctx.action` (the foot). What the kind does at a glance is its hero, beside the status line.
2. Read values through `live(() => setText(...))`; build static structure once.
3. Register it in `PANELS` (and its hero in `HERO`) for the kind(s).
4. If the panel's structure depends on state that the inspector signature does not cover, extend `Inspector.signature()`.
5. i18n keys (`insp.*`) in `fr.ts` + `en.ts`.

**Add a full screen**: see [app.md](app.md) (Recipes). The screen class lives in `src/ui/screens/` and follows the patterns above: build once, `refresh()` with change-only writes, a host for its actions, `data-screen` on its root. Styles go in `screens.css`, using the cartouche classes. A screen with pages implements `back()` so Esc steps back a page before closing it.

**Add a setting**: a field in `Settings` (`src/settings.ts`, with its default), a row in `SettingsScreen` (a `seg(...)` of options, its label keys `settings.*`), a setter in `SettingsHost`, and its implementation in `installMenu` (`src/app/screens.ts`): store, `saveSettings()`, apply.

**Add a HUD element**: create the nodes in the `Hud` constructor and update them in `refresh(nowMs)` with `setText` / `setAttr`. If it triggers something, extend `HudHost` and pass the closure from `App`.

**Add a build-bar entry**: nothing to do in the UI. Trays are derived from content (`trayOf(category)`: every building of the category except the Hearth, in content order), costs come from the building's `cost`, and it appears once its `unlock` is learnt (with a « Nouveau » notice). A building whose `unlock` is `start` is there from the first minute. Its building card follows from content too: write its `desc` well ([Writing a good `desc`](#writing-a-good-desc)).

**Add a figure to the building card**: a row in `kindRows` ([building-facts.ts](../src/ui/building-facts.ts)) for the kind, its label and value keys (`card.*`) in both dictionaries, and an expectation in `building-facts.test.ts`. Read the number from the content tables or `BAL` (never inline), and mirror the engine's formula when research changes it.

## Style

- Tokens: CSS custom properties in `base.css` (`--c-ink`, `--c-paper`, `--c-peach*`, `--c-coral*`, `--c-turq*`, `--c-lilac*`, `--c-sand`, `--c-warn`, glows…), mirrored from `render/art/tokens.ts` by `pushTokensToCss()` at boot, so the DOM and the Pixi art share one palette.
- Fonts: Patrick Hand SC for lettering (`--f-title`, class `.lettering`) and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for text and figures (`--f-body`, tabular numbers). Both are bundled, so they work offline.
- Cartouche: paper fill, an irregular inked border, a hard drop shadow; buttons (`.btn`, `.primary`, `.danger`) press in on `:active`; state is shown with attributes (`data-on`, `data-open`, `data-tone`, `data-poor`, `data-low`), not classes.

## Known gaps

- Keyboard focus management and ARIA are limited to the item picker (listbox), the inspector's focus coming back after a rebuild, and `aria-live` on toasts.
- A language switch from the settings is live for the HUD, the build bar, the inspector, the statistics, every item mention, the menu and its pages; labels that other screens build once in their constructor (parts of the Crier; the tech tree rebuilds itself) keep the old language until the next launch.
- A building's description and CrierBelt's quest messages are content text: they name items in plain words (no icon) unless their author writes a mention token in them (`⟪i:iron_ore|minerai de fer⟫`, [i18n.md](i18n.md#naming-an-item-its-icon-beside-its-name)). Quest labels and guide hints get the icons of their quest's items (`markItems`).
- The cursor tip and the build tray name costs by icon and amount only (the name in the tip's tooltip); the building card, which has the room, names them.
- The UI scale zooms the HUD, the panels and the screens, not the cursor tip; at 130 % on a small window the research and Crier tables can run out of width. The performance mode applies at the next launch only (the renderer is created once).
- The Crier's « Ton flux » and the board's « à tes X/min » follow the market's 5-minute blocks: they lag up to 5 minutes behind a change.
- The tech tree and the HUD's chips each re-derive the tree's state; a shared view-model would avoid that if more screens need it.
- The tech tree's canvas is about 4 400 px wide and 1 100 px tall: the plans fit on a 1 440 px window, the gate and research need panning, sideways and a little downwards (it rebuilds itself on a language switch).
- The specialised views (D88): the Électricité forecast holds the demand and the fuel as they are now (only the weather and the accumulators move); the Logistique heat waits a few seconds after a line is laid (its throughput is sampled); the last view is not remembered between sessions; the Link tool does not open Électricité by itself.
- The body font draws the narrow no-break space (U+202F) at 0,14 em: before « ; » it reads as almost no space (before « % » the sign's own margin shows it).
